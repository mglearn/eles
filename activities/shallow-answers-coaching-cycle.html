The Shallow Answers Coaching Cycle

Coaches role-play a full listen, co-plan, model, measure, adjust cycle on one familiar problem: students finish the digital work but say very little.

Overview

A teacher tells you students are completing every digital activity but their answers are shallow, and some read as if a chatbot wrote them. This simulation walks coaches through a whole coaching cycle on that one case: listening before fixing, co-planning a lesson with fewer questions and more reasoning, modeling a move, and measuring the change in real student work. The cycle ends where the 2026 ELEs say coaching should: "What changed in teacher practice or student evidence?"

Objectives

  • Participants will use listening stems to surface the teacher's goal and evidence before offering any solution.
  • Participants will co-plan a lesson revision that trades question volume for claims supported by evidence and reasoning.
  • Participants will compare before-and-after student work and name a specific change in student evidence and one next adjustment.

Materials

On paper

  • Handout A: Role-Play Cards (1 set per triad, cut apart)
  • Handout B: Student Evidence Samples, Week 1 and Week 3 (1 per person)
  • Handout C: Coaching Cycle Planner (1 per triad)
  • Chart paper with five columns: Listen, Co-plan, Model, Measure, Adjust; sticky notes

On screen

  • Optional: a shared document with Handout C's columns for a virtual session
  • Optional: a timer on the projector

Before you start

  1. Print and cut Handout A. Keep the Teacher Persona cards separate from the Coach Move and Observer cards so each triad can deal them out.
  2. Read Handout B yourself and decide which Week 1 sample you will think aloud about in the Model step.
  3. Post the five-column chart where every triad can reach it with sticky notes.

Step by step

  1. 10–5 min

    Hook

    Same question, two answers

    Read two answers from Handout B aloud: Week 1 Sample 2 ("Plants need sunlight to grow so the plant with more light grew more") and Week 3 Sample 2. Ask: "Both students finished the task. Which one gives you evidence of learning, and how do you know?" Take three responses. Say: "Completion was never the problem. Today we coach toward the second answer, one cycle at a time."

    Facilitator noteListen for people who reach straight for a new tool or a new rubric. Name that pull gently: it is the urge we practice resisting in the Listen phase.

  2. 25–17 min

    Explore

    Listen first (round 1 role-play)

    Triads deal the cards: one person takes a Teacher Persona card, one takes the Coach Move: Listen card, one takes the Observer card. The teacher opens with the line on the persona card. The coach may only use the listening stems for six minutes: no suggestions, no tools, no "have you tried." The observer tallies questions vs. suggestions and writes down the teacher's goal in the teacher's own words. Then the observer reports for two minutes and the triad posts one sticky note in the Listen column: "The teacher's real goal is…"

    Facilitator noteThe persona cards hide a detail (for example, the teacher suspects students paste chatbot answers but hasn't said so). Good listening surfaces it. If a coach gives advice in the first two minutes, the observer should flag it with a raised hand.

  3. 317–25 min

    Model

    Think aloud: co-planning fewer, better questions

    Using Week 1 Sample 4 from Handout B, think aloud as the coach co-planning with the teacher. Say what you notice ("The answer is fluent but never mentions our plant data"), then co-plan out loud: cut the 12-question digital activity to three questions, each requiring a claim, a piece of the class's own data, and a "because" sentence that links them. Add one AI check: students may ask an approved chatbot for an explanation only after they write their own, then must mark one thing the chatbot said that their data supports and one thing it did not. Record the plan on the chart under Co-plan and Model.

    Facilitator notePoint out that the revised lesson keeps the device. It changes what the device is used to produce: a visible chain from data to claim.

  4. 425–38 min

    Practice

    Co-plan and model (round 2 role-play)

    Rotate roles. The new coach draws a Coach Move: Co-plan card and a Coach Move: Model card. Using Handout C, the triad co-plans a revision for their persona's lesson (not the science lesson you modeled). The coach then models one move for two minutes as if in front of students: a think-aloud, a sentence frame, or a verification check. The teacher decides what to keep. The observer checks that the plan names what student evidence will look different next week.

    Facilitator noteIf a triad writes "students will be more engaged," ask: "What would you see on paper?" Engagement is not the evidence. Claims, reasons, and data references are.

  5. 538–48 min

    Apply

    Measure and adjust

    Everyone reads the Week 3 samples on Handout B, which come from the same class after a revision like the one you modeled. Triads count, for each sample, whether it has a claim, class data, and reasoning that links them, then tally the Week 1 samples the same way. Rotate roles once more: the final coach draws Coach Move: Measure and Adjust and leads a four-minute conversation with the teacher on what changed and what to try next. Post one sticky under Measure and one under Adjust.

    Facilitator noteWeek 3 is better, not perfect: Sample 5 still leans on the chatbot's wording, and Sample 3 has data but no reasoning. Strong triads adjust for those two students, not the whole class.

  6. 648–56 min

    Debrief

    What changed in teacher practice or student evidence?

    Walk the five-column chart from left to right. Ask: "Where did we feel the most pull to skip ahead? What did the listening phase surface that the plan needed?" Then ask the coaching question directly: "What changed in teacher practice, and what changed in student evidence?" Have each triad answer both halves in one sentence each.

    Facilitator noteMost rooms discover that the Listen phase is where the AI concern came out. That's worth naming: teachers often raise the problem they are comfortable naming first.

  7. 756–60 min

    Transfer

    Pick your real teacher

    Each coach writes the name of one teacher they will start a cycle with in the next two weeks on the back of Handout C, plus the first listening stem they'll use. Teachers in the room write one lesson where they'll trade question volume for reasoning.

    Facilitator noteKeep it private. The commitment matters more than sharing it.

Paper or screen

Unplugged

This activity is designed to run with no devices. The role-play cards, printed student samples, and the planner carry the whole cycle. The measurement step uses a hand tally on Handout B, which is exactly what a coach and teacher can do at a table with a stack of exit tickets.

Digital

In a virtual session, put triads in breakout rooms, share the role-play cards as a slide deck with one card per slide, and give each triad a shared copy of Handout C. Paste the student samples into a document triads can highlight in three colors (claim, data, reasoning). A shared board with the five cycle columns replaces the chart paper.

Does it need a screen? Unplugged is the better default here: the skill is a conversation, and screens pull attention away from the partner. A shared digital planner earns its place only when the real cycle continues over weeks and the coach and teacher need one record of what they tried and what student work showed.

Evidence of learning

What you should be able to see or collect if it worked.

  • In the Listen round, observers' tallies show more questions than suggestions, and the teacher's goal is recorded in the teacher's words.
  • Each co-planned revision on Handout C names what student evidence will look different, not just what the teacher will do.
  • Triads tally claim, data, and reasoning across Week 1 and Week 3 and name at least one student who needs a different adjustment than the class.

Adaptations

K–2 teachers
Swap Handout B for oral responses you transcribe: "I think ___ because I saw ___." Measure whether students name something they observed.
Higher Ed faculty
Use the same protocol with discussion-board posts. The revision cuts required posts from three to one, with a claim tied to a course reading and a reply that tests a peer's evidence.
New coaches
Run only the Listen round twice with different persona cards before moving on. It is the hardest phase to do well.

Reflect

  • How effectively am I supporting teachers in improving their instructional practices? (Coach ELE guiding question)
  • In my last coaching conversation, how much of the time was I listening versus suggesting?
  • What student evidence would convince me that a co-planned change worked, and when will I look at it with the teacher?

Take it to your students

Start a real cycle with the teacher you named: one listening conversation this week, a co-planned lesson next week, and a 15-minute look at the student work together afterward. Bring the before-and-after tally to your coaching team.
