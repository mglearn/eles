Ask Two

A confident robot helper says things that are sometimes right and sometimes wrong, and children become learning owners by checking each answer in two places before they believe it.

Overview

Talking speakers, apps, and AI chatbots answer children's questions in a friendly, sure-sounding voice. Sounding sure is not the same as being right. In this lesson, Robo Helper (you, reading answer cards, plus an optional projected AI chatbot) gives answers to simple science and everyday questions. Pairs pick up a card and Ask Two: they check it at two of three stations (Book Nook, Look and See, Grown-Up Desk) before deciding right, wrong, or not sure yet. Children practice owning their learning: asking, checking, and changing their minds when the evidence says so.

Objectives

  • Students will check a robot helper's answer in two places (a book, their own eyes, or a trusted grown-up) before deciding if it is right.
  • Students will say that a computer can sound sure and still be wrong.
  • Students will record their two checks and their decision by drawing or writing on an Ask Two card.
  • Students will name one way they are good at checking and one thing they want to get better at.

Materials

On paper

  • Handout A: Robo Helper Says… cards (1 set per 5 pairs, cut apart)
  • Handout B: My Ask Two Card (2 per pair)
  • Handout C: I Am a Learning Owner checklist (1 per student)
  • Station signs: BOOK NOOK, LOOK AND SEE, GROWN-UP DESK
  • Book Nook: 4–6 simple nonfiction books about spiders, frogs, butterflies, penguins, whales, plants, and the sun and Earth
  • Look and See: a cup with an ice cube, a small potted plant (or a photo of a watered and a dried plant), a wall calendar, a picture of a spider
  • Grown-Up Desk: you, an aide, a volunteer, or an older reading buddy with the card key

On screen

  • Optional: a generative AI chatbot your district approves, on the teacher's device only, projected for the class
  • Optional: a kid-friendly nonfiction website or e-book your library provides, as a fourth "source" projected by the teacher

Before you start

  1. Cut apart the Robo Helper cards. Print the backs as a key for the Grown-Up Desk.
  2. Pull nonfiction books from the library that answer the cards' questions and mark the right pages with sticky notes so young readers can find them.
  3. Set up the Look and See station. Put an ice cube in a cup 10 minutes before the lesson so it has started to melt.
  4. If you'll run the projected demo, test your district-approved chatbot first with the questions listed in the Digital section.

Step by step

  1. 10–4 min

    Hook

    Robo Helper sounds very sure

    In a proud robot voice, read: "Robo Helper says: A spider has 6 legs. I am 100 percent sure!" Ask: "Robo sounds SO sure. Do we believe it?" Let children react. Hold up the spider picture and count the legs together: 8. Say: "Robo sounded sure, but it was wrong. Sounding sure is not the same as being right. Today you'll learn a superpower called Ask Two."

    Facilitator noteIf a child already knows spiders have 8 legs, great: ask "How do you know? Where did you learn it?" That's a source.

  2. 24–10 min

    Model

    Ask Two: think aloud

    Hold up two fingers: "Before I believe it, I check it in TWO places." Introduce the three stations: Book Nook (look in a book), Look and See (check with your own eyes), Grown-Up Desk (ask a trusted grown-up). Model with the card "Ice melts when it gets warm": go to Look and See ("The ice cube in the cup is melting. Check one!"), then Book Nook (show a page). Fill in a My Ask Two Card on the document camera: Robo said, check 1, check 2, circle RIGHT.

    Facilitator notePoint out that you checked even though Robo was right. Checking isn't only for wrong answers; it's how learners own what they know.

  3. 310–24 min

    Practice

    Checking stations

    Each pair draws a Robo Helper card and gets a My Ask Two Card. Pairs choose two stations that fit their question, visit them, and draw or write what they found. Then they circle RIGHT, WRONG, or NOT SURE YET. When done, they draw a new card. Circulate and ask: "Which station helped most for your question? Why?" At the Grown-Up Desk, the adult doesn't give the answer right away but asks, "Where could we look?" and helps the pair find it.

    Facilitator note"Not sure yet" is a great answer when two checks disagree or can't be found. Praise it. It's honest and it means you keep checking.

  4. 424–30 min

    Debrief

    What did Robo get wrong?

    Gather on the rug. Pairs share one card: "Robo said ___. We checked ___ and ___. Robo was ___." Tally on the board how many Robo answers were right and how many were wrong. Ask: "Could you tell which ones were wrong just by listening to Robo's voice?" (No, Robo always sounded sure.) Then: "Some computers use AI to answer questions. They can be helpful and they can be wrong. A learning owner does what?" Children hold up two fingers: "Ask Two!"

    Facilitator noteIf you run the projected demo, do it here, before the tally, and add the real chatbot's answers to the checking.

  5. 530–35 min

    Reflect

    I am a learning owner

    Read the Handout C checklist aloud, one line at a time. Children color Yes or Not yet. Then each child finishes the last two lines of their My Ask Two Card with a drawing or a few words: "I am good at checking by…" and "Next time I will…" Ask two or three children to share their "Next time" goal.

    Facilitator noteThis is S4.3 in action: children notice a strength and a next step. Keep goals small ("I will look in a book first").

Paper or screen

Unplugged

Robo Helper can be entirely you, reading the cards in a robot voice. The books, real objects, and adults at the stations are the sources, so no device is needed. The lesson loses nothing essential without the projected demo.

Digital

During the Debrief, project a district-approved AI chatbot from your device. Let children pick two or three of the same questions ("How many legs does a spider have?" "Can penguins fly?" "Where do pumpkins grow?") and type them as the class watches, adding "Answer for a first grader." Read each answer aloud, then Ask Two as a class using a book and a projected library e-book or kid-friendly site. Whether the chatbot is right or wrong, the move is the same: check in two places. Children never use their own AI accounts; you type every question.

Does it need a screen? The projected chatbot makes the lesson real: children see that an actual AI tool answers in a smooth, sure voice, and they practice checking it with books and adults. The evidence of learning is the Ask Two card with two checks recorded, not the chatbot's answer.

Evidence of learning

What you should be able to see or collect if it worked.

  • My Ask Two Cards show two different checks (book, eyes, grown-up) and a circled decision that matches the evidence.
  • Children change their decision when a check disagrees with Robo, or choose "not sure yet" when their checks disagree.
  • In the Debrief, children say that a sure-sounding voice doesn't prove an answer is right.
  • On the Ask Two Card, children name a real checking strength and a small next-step goal.

Adaptations

Kindergarten
Run the stations as a whole class, one card at a time, with you walking the class to each station. Children circle a thumbs-up or thumbs-down face instead of writing.
Grade 2
Add a rule: at least one check must be a book or other printed source. Pairs write one sentence explaining which source they trusted most and why.
Emergent bilinguals
Provide nonfiction books in the home language when you can, and let the Grown-Up Desk adult speak the child's home language if available.
Early readers
Put a sticky-note picture cue on each marked book page, and pair emergent readers with a stronger reader for the Book Nook.

Standards connections

Children check a robot helper's claims in a book, by observing, or with a grown-up, early inquiry and scientific observation that shows sure-sounding answers can be wrong.

Reflect

  • Am I trying my best to learn on my own? How did I check today? (Student ELE guiding question)
  • Which station helped you most? Why?
  • What will you do the next time a computer or speaker tells you something?

What comes next

Send home a blank My Ask Two Card. Families pick something a speaker, app, or TV show told them this week and check it in two places together (a book, a grown-up, or looking with their own eyes). In class, post "Ask Two" by the classroom library and use the two-finger signal whenever a surprising fact comes up.
