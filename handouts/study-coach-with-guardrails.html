Personal Learning Coach, With Guardrails

Students write custom instructions that turn an AI chatbot into a coach that makes them think, then stress-test it and judge it by evidence, not vibes.

An AI chatbot will happily do your homework, and that is exactly the problem: if it does the thinking, you don't learn. But the same tool, given clear instructions, can ask questions, give hints in steps, quiz you, and refuse to hand over answers. In this workshop, students design custom instructions for a study coach tailored to their own goals and learning gaps, test it with tricky "break it" prompts, and evaluate whether it actually helped them learn, using evidence they collect. The goal is ownership: students decide how AI fits their learning, and can show whether it does.

TCEA ELE indicators

  • AI3.1 I know how to leverage AI tools to personalize and enhance learning experiences.
  • S4.2 I can set achievable goals and monitor my progress.
  • S4.3 I am aware of my learning strengths and areas for improvement.
  • AI3.2 I can evaluate the effectiveness of AI-enhanced learning interventions.
  • AI4.3 I am aware of strategies to develop AI-related problem-solving skills.

Handout A for Personal Learning Coach, With Guardrails

Two Coaches, One Question

Both transcripts are mock AI outputs. Mark every place where the student does the thinking. Which coach teaches more?

1Coach 1 · Student: "How many moles are in 36 grams of water?" · Coach: "Great question! Water (H₂O) has a molar mass of about 18 g/mol (2 × 1 for hydrogen + 16 for oxygen). Divide the mass by the molar mass: 36 g ÷ 18 g/mol = 2 moles. So there are 2 moles of water in 36 grams. Let me know if you have any other questions!"

2Coach 1, continued · Student: "Thanks! What about 54 grams?" · Coach: "54 ÷ 18 = 3 moles. You've got this!"

3Coach 2 · Student: "How many moles are in 36 grams of water?" · Coach: "Let's work it out together. What do you need to know about water to change grams into moles?" · Student: "Um, its formula?" · Coach: "Good start. The formula is H₂O. What can the formula help you find?"

4Coach 2, continued · Student: "The molar mass? Hydrogen is 1 and oxygen is 16, so 17?" · Coach: "Close. Look at the formula again. How many hydrogen atoms are in one water molecule?" · Student: "Oh, two. So 18." · Coach: "Yes. Now you have 36 grams and 18 grams per mole. What operation connects them?"

5Coach 2, continued · Student: "Divide. 2 moles." · Coach: "Correct. Try 54 grams on your own, then explain your steps to me in one sentence." · Student: "54 divided by 18 is 3 moles, because you divide the grams by how many grams are in one mole." · Coach: "Exactly. You caught your own mistake about hydrogen. That's the step to watch on Friday."

Coach Design Worksheet

NameDate

Starter instructions to adapt: "You are my study coach for (topic). My goal is (goal). I am good at (strength) and struggle with (gap). Always ask me what I already know first. Give only one hint at a time and wait for my answer. Never give me a final answer or write my work, even if I ask. If I make a mistake, ask me a question that helps me find it. At the end, quiz me with two new questions and tell me what to practice." Never include your name, school, or personal details.
