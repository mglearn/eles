Before It Happens: The Family Talk

A campus team builds, and sends home, a calm, trauma-informed guide that helps families talk with their teen about AI deepfake images and sextortion before a crisis, leading with "come to me, you won't be in trouble" instead of fear.

Families often hear about AI deepfake image abuse and sextortion only after it hits their child or makes the news, and by then fear drives the conversation. This session helps a campus team get ahead of it by building a short, calm, trauma-informed family guide and a plan to get it into homes in the languages families use. Teams learn the essentials, what these images are, that sextortion schemes target teens, that it is never the young person's fault, and what the law and takedown options now provide, and then translate that into warm, plain language a parent can actually use at the kitchen table. The anchor message is relationship, not fear: the single most protective thing a family can do is make sure a teen believes "if this ever happens, come to me and you will not be in trouble." Teams draft conversation starters, a what-to-do-tonight box, and a resource list, and avoid the common traps of scaring teens, blaming victims, or burying the one message that matters.

Handout A for Before It Happens: The Family Talk

What Families Need to Know

Read with your team. Highlight the 3–4 points that matter most for YOUR families. This is background, not legal advice; verify local specifics.

1What it is: Apps can fabricate realistic nude or sexual images of a real person who never posed. When done to a teen, it is image-based sexual abuse, not a prank, and it can be a crime even when a minor made it.

2Sextortion is rising and targets teens: In sextortion, someone threatens to share real or fake intimate images unless the victim sends money or more images. It targets girls and, very often, boys, frequently through financial schemes. The pressure is designed to make the teen panic and stay silent.

3It is never the young person's fault: Whether an image is real or an AI fake, the blame lies with the person who made or shared or threatened, never the target. This is the belief that lets a teen come forward.

4There is a real path: The 2025 federal Take It Down Act makes non-consensual sharing of intimate images, including deepfakes, a crime and requires platforms to remove reported material quickly. Free services help remove images of minors from participating platforms.

5The steps if it happens: Don't pay and don't comply with threats; save proof of where it is without forwarding it; tell a trusted adult; report to the platform and, with an adult, to law enforcement or the national reporting line; and request takedown. Get emotional support, not just logistics.

6The most protective thing a family can do: Make sure, before anything happens, that the teen knows, "If anyone ever does this to you or threatens you, come to me. You will not be in trouble. I will help you." Shame and fear of punishment are exactly what keep teens silent and in danger.
