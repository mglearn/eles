The AI Critic Tank

Teams design a solution to a local community problem by hand, invite an AI chatbot to tear it apart (as critic, never author), decide which critiques to accept, disclose the AI's role, and pitch to a panel.

When students use AI on a project, it too often writes the idea and the student decorates it. This session flips the roles. Teams of teachers, working as students, choose a problem in the fictional town of Cedar Bend, understand it, and design a solution entirely on their own. Only then do they ask an approved chatbot to act as a skeptical critic. They log every critique, accept, reject, or modify each one with a reason, revise, write an honest disclosure of how AI was used, and pitch to a "Tank" panel. The debrief turns the structure into a problem-solving project teachers can run with students.

Handout A for The AI Critic Tank

Cedar Bend Problem Cards

Each team chooses one problem. Cedar Bend and everyone in it are fictional. Card backs are facilitator hints: print them separately.

Problem 1 · Transportation

Many Cedar Bend High students can't stay for after-school tutoring or clubs because the late bus was cut and their families work until 6 p.m.

Problem 2 · Food waste

The Cedar Bend Elementary cafeteria throws away large amounts of untouched fruit and milk each day, while the town food pantry runs low by midweek.

Problem 3 · Park safety

Families avoid Riverside Park after dark because the lights along the walking trail are broken, and the city says repairs are months away.

Problem 4 · Library access

The Cedar Bend Public Library closes at 5 p.m. on weekdays, and many students without home internet have nowhere to finish online assignments.

Problem 5 · Newcomer families

Newly arrived families say school messages are confusing, arrive in English only, and use terms like "STAAR" and "early release" without explanation.

Problem 6 · Heat

The Cedar Bend Middle School playground has almost no shade, and recess is regularly moved indoors during the hottest months.

Problem 7 · Online conflict

Arguments in a popular Cedar Bend students' group chat keep spilling into school, and many students say they don't know how to step away or get help.

Problem 8 · Senior isolation

Many older residents at the Maple Court apartments rarely leave home and say they feel lonely and unsure how to use video calls to reach family.

Answer key and notes

Handout A: Cedar Bend Problem Cards

Problem 1 · Transportation
Often-missed stakeholders: students who care for younger siblings, bus drivers, club sponsors, students with part-time jobs.
Problem 2 · Food waste
Often-missed stakeholders: cafeteria staff, the health inspector, pantry volunteers, families who use the pantry and may not want to be identified.
Problem 3 · Park safety
Often-missed stakeholders: older adults who walk in the evening, people who work late shifts, neighbors near the trail, city maintenance staff.
Problem 4 · Library access
Often-missed stakeholders: library staff and budget, students without devices, teachers who assign online work, local businesses with Wi-Fi.
Problem 5 · Newcomer families
Often-missed stakeholders: students who translate for their parents, front-office staff, community interpreters, families who speak languages other than Spanish.
Problem 6 · Heat
Often-missed stakeholders: students with health conditions, PE teachers, custodial staff, the PTA, the district facilities department.
Problem 7 · Online conflict
Often-missed stakeholders: bystanders in the chat, students who are targeted, counselors, families, students who run the chat.
Problem 8 · Senior isolation
Often-missed stakeholders: apartment staff, residents' families, residents with hearing or vision loss, student volunteers and their schedules.
