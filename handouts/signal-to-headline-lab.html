Signal to Headline Lab

Students turn a true present-day tech trend into a plausible future headline and lede, then trade and fact-check each other's work for accuracy and clickbait.

Futures researchers watch for signals: small, real pieces of evidence that something is changing. In this lab, student pairs draw a signal card describing something true about technology today, decide where it could plausibly lead, and write the news story from that future as a journalist would: a headline, a lede, and an evidence line. Then pairs trade and fact-check each other's work. Does the headline match the story? Does it trace back to something real, or is it just possible? Is it news or clickbait? Students finish by proposing one rule or habit their generation should adopt now.

Handout C for Signal to Headline Lab

Headline Fact-Check

NameDate

Check the other pair's draft. Then write one specific fix on their Handout B, next to the headline.

YesPartlyNo
The headline says the same thing the lede says. It doesn't promise something the story doesn't deliver.
The headline names who and what, not a vague "they" or "everyone."
No clickbait cues: all caps, piles of exclamation points, "you won't believe," "shocking," or a question the story never answers.
The lede answers who, what, when, where, and why.
The evidence line points to something that really exists today (it matches the signal card).
The future is plausible because it follows from the signal. It isn't just possible.
No invented statistics, numbers, or quotes presented as fact.
People, places, and companies are fictional.
The draft is clearly labeled FICTION FROM THE FUTURE, so no one could mistake it for real news.
The story shows at least one person who benefits and one who could be left out.

Answer key and notes

Handout A: Signal Cards

Signal 1
True now: voice-cloning tools exist, and scammers have used cloned voices to pretend to be family members. Plausible directions: families or schools adopt verification habits; laws about using someone's voice without permission.
Signal 2
True now: image and video generators are widely available, and fake images have spread during breaking news. Plausible directions: labels on made-with-AI images, school discipline policies about fake images of classmates, verification classes.
Signal 3
True now: live translation features exist on some devices, with accuracy that varies by language and accent. Plausible directions: newcomer students join class discussions sooner; debate over whether learning languages still matters.
Signal 4
True now: generative AI produces fluent text and can "hallucinate" facts, sources, and quotes. Plausible directions: more in-class and oral assessment; AI disclosure notes on schoolwork; fact-checking taught earlier.
Signal 5
True now: AI companion apps exist, and lawmakers and child advocates have raised concerns about young users. Plausible directions: age rules, school counseling guidance, debates about loneliness and friendship.
Signal 6
True now: content-credential and provenance labeling efforts exist, though they are not yet used everywhere. Plausible directions: "check the label" becomes a habit; unlabeled images get treated with more doubt.
Signal 7
True now: phone restrictions in schools have spread and are actively debated. Plausible directions: new rules for smartwatches and smart glasses; arguments about emergencies and accessibility exceptions.
Signal 8
True now: multimodal AI assistants can describe scenes, and assistive apps help people who are blind or have low vision. Plausible directions: new independence for some students; privacy questions about cameras pointed at others.
Signal 9
True now: recommendation algorithms personalize feeds based on behavior. Plausible directions: laws requiring a chronological option for teens; students auditing their own feeds; debates about who controls what people see.
Signal 10
True now: AI "agents" that can click and type on websites are being released and tested. Plausible directions: questions about who is responsible when an agent makes a mistake or is tricked by a scam.
Signal 11
True now: data-center energy and water use is a public concern in some communities. Plausible directions: local votes on new data centers; students weighing the environmental cost of AI use.
Signal 12
True now: tutoring-style AI tools exist and some schools are piloting them. Plausible directions: schools choose tutor-style tools over answer tools; debates about whether students without access fall behind.
