Professional learning · Coaches, Librarians, Leaders · 60 minutes

Four-Lens App Review

Teams vet three fictional classroom apps through four lenses (learning value, privacy, accessibility, AI transparency) and turn the verdict into a pilot a teacher can actually run.

Teachers forward coaches a steady stream of "Can we use this?" messages, and more of the tools now include AI features that write, grade, or talk to students. In this protocol, teams review fictional app profiles and policy excerpts through four lenses, cite the exact line behind every score, and practice reading vendor claims laterally rather than taking them at face value. The session ends with a decision and a pilot plan built around the 2026 ELE question: what student evidence will show the tool improved the learning?

In this packet

  • Handout A: App Profiles: Three Fictional Tools (reading)
  • Handout B: Four-Lens Rubric (rubric)
  • Handout C: Recommendation and Pilot Plan (worksheet)

TCEA ELE indicators

  • C2.2 I can evaluate the effectiveness of educational technology tools.
  • AI2.3 I am aware of the importance of transparency and accountability in AI systems.
  • C2.1 I know how to guide teachers in meaningful technology integration.
  • T3.2 I can adapt my teaching methods to accommodate diverse learning needs.

Handout A for Four-Lens App Review

App Profiles: Three Fictional Tools

All apps, companies, and quotes are fictional. Read your assigned profile. Quote the exact line behind every rubric score and star any claim you would verify elsewhere.

1QuizNova (grades 6–12), from Nova Learning Labs. Marketing page: "Paste any article, video link, or document and our proprietary AI builds a 20-question quiz in seconds. 100% accurate, curriculum-aligned questions. Teachers save hours every week!" Features: multiple-choice and short answer; automatic grading of short answers with AI-written feedback to students.

2QuizNova privacy excerpt: "Content uploaded by teachers and responses submitted by students may be used to improve our AI models. Districts may request an opt-out by contacting support. We retain student responses for as long as the account is active." Accessibility page: "QuizNova works with most screen readers." No information on how the AI grades short answers or how teachers can review AI feedback before students see it.

3StoryBloom (grades K–2), from Bloomfield Kids Inc. Marketing page: "An AI reading buddy that listens to your child read aloud and responds in a friendly voice! Builds fluency and confidence. Student Privacy Certified." Features: children read stories aloud; the app records their voice and an AI character gives spoken encouragement and corrections.

4StoryBloom terms excerpt: "StoryBloom is not intended for children under 13 without verified consent from a parent or school. Voice recordings are stored to improve speech recognition and may be reviewed by our quality team." Accessibility: all stories include audio and text highlighting; no information on support for students who use speech devices or who speak languages other than English at home. The "Student Privacy Certified" badge does not link anywhere.

5PeerLoop (grades 6–12 and Higher Ed), from Loopwise Education. Marketing page: "Students give each other feedback on drafts using structured sentence stems. Optional AI Assist suggests a kinder or more specific way to phrase a comment. Teachers see every comment and every AI suggestion." Features: teacher-built rubrics; students must accept, edit, or reject AI Assist suggestions, and the history is saved.

6PeerLoop privacy excerpt: "We do not use student content to train AI models. AI Assist is provided by a third-party model provider under a contract that prohibits retention of student data. Districts can turn AI Assist off. Data is deleted within 60 days of contract end." Accessibility: "Tested with keyboard-only navigation and major screen readers; captions on all videos. Our most recent accessibility conformance report is available on request."

7What every profile leaves out. None of the three vendors offers evidence about student learning beyond their own marketing. Your pilot plan will have to generate that evidence.
