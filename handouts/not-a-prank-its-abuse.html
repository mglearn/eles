Not a Prank, It's Abuse

Students face the hard truth that making or sharing an AI nude of a real classmate is image-based sexual abuse and a crime, learn what to do if they or a friend are targeted, and rehearse being the person who refuses to pass it on.

Apps that fabricate realistic nude images of real people have turned up in high schools, and the harm often hides behind words like prank or joke. This lesson strips that cover away. Without ever showing or describing explicit material, students learn that creating or sharing a non-consensual sexual image of a real person, including an AI deepfake, is image-based sexual abuse and is illegal, with the 2025 federal Take It Down Act now requiring platforms to remove it fast. They work through what to do if they are targeted (it is not your fault, save proof without sharing, tell a trusted adult, report, request takedown) and, just as important, how to act when they are a bystander, the person in the group chat who can refuse to forward it, report it, and support the target instead of laughing along. The tone is serious, trauma-informed, and non-shaming; the goal is a school where students understand the real stakes and know that the strong move is to stop the harm, not spread it.

Making or sharing a fake sexual image of a real person is image-based sexual abuse.

This can be a crime even when both people involved are minors.

"Once it's online there's nothing anyone can do."

The person targeted is a victim and it is not their fault.

Telling a trusted adult and reporting it is the strong move, not snitching.
