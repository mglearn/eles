Student learning · Grades 3–5, Grades 6–8 · 45 minutes

Next Word, By Hand

Students build a paper "word-guessing machine" from sentence cards, watch it invent a confident false fact about a lake, and find out why a longer prompt helps but never makes a chatbot know things.

A generative AI chatbot writes by guessing what word is likely to come next, based on patterns in the writing it learned from. In this game, teams become that machine. They tally which word follows which in eight short sentence cards, then "write" new sentences by drawing slips from cups. The sentences come out grammatical and often silly. Then, in the lake round, the machine produces a sentence that sounds like a fact and is false, even though every piece of it came from true cards. Students test whether a longer prompt fixes the problem and leave able to say, in their own words, why a chatbot can sound sure and still be wrong.

Handout C for Next Word, By Hand

How Did Our Machine Write That?

NameDate

Answer on your own after the lake round. Use your own words. Then share one answer with a partner.

  1. The silliest sentence our machine wrote was:

  2. Our machine wrote something false about a lake. No card said it. Where did it come from?

  3. When we gave the machine three words instead of two, what changed? What could a longer prompt NOT fix?

  4. Finish the sentence: "A chatbot can sound sure and still be wrong because…"

  5. When a chatbot tells me a fact, I can check it by…

  6. Draw your paper machine at work: the cups, a slip, and a sentence coming out.

Facilitator only for Next Word, By Hand

Answer key and notes

Handout A: Sentence Cards

Story 1
Adds: the→dog, dog→ran, ran→to, to→the, the→park. Model this one with the whole class.
Story 2
Second slip after "dog" (ate), and the first "ate→the."
Story 3
Second "ran→to." The ran cup will hold only "to" slips: a sure-thing guess.
Story 4
Introduces sat→on and on→the.
Story 5
Third "ran→to." The the cup keeps getting more different words.
Story 6
Watch for generated sentences like "the bench ran to the apple." Grammar right, meaning wrong.
Story 7
Adds ate→the and the→apple.
Story 8
After this card, the the cup has 16 slips with 11 different words. That mix is where the silly sentences come from.
Lake 9
True in our cards. With three words ("Wrenmere is in"), the only next word is Harlow.
Lake 10
With only two words ("is in"), the machine can't tell which lake it's talking about, so "Wrenmere is in Pike County" becomes possible.
Lake 11
Source of the hallucination: "is the" can be followed by "deepest."
Lake 12
Mixed with card 11, two-word guessing can produce "Mossmere is the deepest lake in the state." Every piece is true; the sentence is false.
