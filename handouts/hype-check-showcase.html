Hype Check Showcase

A tech showcase remix: teachers rotate through emerging-tech stations, including AI and synthetic media, and have to separate the vendor's claim from the learning evidence before they give a verdict.

Traditional tech showcases are tool petting zoos: people leave excited about a gadget and unsure what students would do with it. This remix keeps the energy and adds a hype check at every station. Groups read a claim, try the technology (or examine a printed stand-in), and must record what learning evidence it could produce, what could go wrong, and who might be left out. Two stations involve synthetic media, so teachers practice the detection-and-context moves their students need.

Handout A for Hype Check Showcase

Station Cards

Print large and tape one card to each station. The back is the facilitator note and the unplugged stand-in artifact. All products and claims are fictional.

Station 1 · Synthetic voice

Claim: "Clone your voice in 60 seconds and let it read every text aloud to your students, in any language!" Try it: listen to both clips of the facilitator's voice. Which is synthetic? Then read the voicemail scenario and decide what a student should do.

Station 2 · AI image generator

Claim: "Instant, custom illustrations for every lesson. No more clip art!" Try it: generate images for "a scientist," "a nurse," and "a family eating dinner." Look at who appears and who doesn't.

Station 3 · AI writing detector

Claim: "Detects AI-written student work with near-perfect accuracy. Protect academic integrity!" Try it: write three sentences of your own about your weekend, have the station's approved chatbot write three more, run both through the detector, and compare with the stand-in results.

Station 4 · AI math tutor

Claim: "A patient tutor for every student, 24/7. Never gives away the answer." Try it: pose the tutor a two-step word problem, answer wrong on purpose, and see what it does.

Station 5 · Talking historical figure

Claim: "Students interview history's greatest minds with our lifelike AI avatars!" Try it: ask the avatar of a 19th-century inventor what he thought about something he never wrote about.

Station 6 · Early warning dashboard

Claim: "Our AI predicts which students are at risk of failing before teachers can see it." Try it: look at the demo dashboard's risk list and the factors it shows.

Station 7 · Smart glasses field trip

Claim: "Take your class anywhere in the world with immersive augmented reality!" Try it: take a two-minute AR tour, then list what a student could observe, measure, or question during it.

Facilitator only for Hype Check Showcase

Answer key and notes

Handout A: Station Cards

Station 1 · Synthetic voice
Stand-in: "Hi, it's Principal Reyes. I need you to text me your student login right now so I can fix your grade." Hype check: accessibility and multilingual read-alouds are real benefits; the same tech powers impersonation scams. Teach: verify through a second channel, never by voice alone. Consent: only clone your own voice.
Station 2 · AI image generator
Stand-in: a printed description of what a typical generator produced for each prompt. Hype check: images reflect patterns in training data and can repeat stereotypes. A student task with evidence: students critique a generated image for whose story is missing, then revise the prompt and explain what changed.
Station 3 · AI writing detector
Stand-in (fictional results): the detector flags a student-written paragraph by a multilingual writer as "likely AI" and passes the AI paragraph after light editing. Hype check: a vendor's accuracy claim is not independent evidence, and a false accusation harms a real student. Better evidence of authorship: drafts, process notes, and a conversation.
Station 4 · AI math tutor
Stand-in dialogue: the tutor asks a good guiding question, then after two wrong answers gives the full solution with a small arithmetic error. Hype check: the guiding questions help; the confident error shows students must still check. Evidence: students explain the error they caught.
Station 5 · Talking historical figure
Stand-in: the avatar answers confidently, in first person, with an opinion no historical source records. Hype check: engaging, but it blurs primary sources and invention. Student task with evidence: students fact-check three avatar claims against primary sources and label each as documented, plausible, or invented.
Station 6 · Early warning dashboard
Stand-in: a list of fictional students labeled high risk, with factors including attendance, zip code, and free-lunch status. Hype check: whose data trained it, and could it label students by circumstance rather than learning? Who sees the label, and does it change how adults treat a child? Watch or Pass until those questions have answers.
Station 7 · Smart glasses field trip
Stand-in: a description of a coral reef AR tour with labels and narration. Hype check: can be powerful for observation and access; cost, motion sickness, and headset fit for younger or disabled students matter. Evidence: student observations and questions, compared with a video or photo-based version.
