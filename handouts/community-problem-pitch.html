Community Problem Pitch

Teams use the engineering design process to solve a real school or community problem, invite AI to critique their ideas, reject what doesn't hold up, and pitch a solution with a record of what changed.

Students pick a real problem they can observe at school or in their community, gather evidence, and generate their own ideas before any AI is involved. Then AI joins as a brainstorm critic: the teacher projects a district-approved chatbot (or uses a printed mock critique) that suggests ideas and pokes holes in the team's plan. Some of its suggestions are useful, some are unrealistic, and some include claims no one can verify. Teams must decide what to keep, verify, or reject, build a simple prototype, and pitch it. The grade rests on their reasoning and their record of what changed, not on the AI's ideas.

Handout A for Community Problem Pitch

Sample AI Critic Response

This mock AI response was written for this lesson. Ridgeview Middle School is fictional. For each suggestion or weakness, decide: Keep, Verify, or Reject, and say why.

1The team's prompt: "You are a friendly but tough critic for a middle school design team. Our problem: a lot of food gets thrown away at lunch at our school. We counted 23 full or half-full trays thrown away in five minutes. Our idea: make posters asking students not to waste food. Give us three suggestions and three weaknesses in our plan."

2AI response, part 1 (Suggestions): Great problem to tackle! Here are three ideas. 1. Start a share table. Students could place unopened items, like packaged snacks or whole fruit, on a table for others to take. Share tables are allowed in every school, so you can start right away. 2. Let students choose portion sizes. Some students may throw food away because they get more than they want. A "small or regular" choice at the serving line could help. 3. Start a composting program. Studies show that composting reduces cafeteria waste by 90%, so this is the most effective solution.

3AI response, part 2 (Bonus ideas): You could also ban packed lunches so every student eats the same food and the cafeteria can plan exact amounts. Or you could install AI cameras above the trash cans that scan every tray and send students a weekly report of how much they wasted.

4AI response, part 3 (Weaknesses in your plan): 1. Posters alone may not change what people do, because students might ignore them after the first few days. 2. You counted trays, but you don't know why the food was thrown away. Was it taste, time, or portion size? Interviewing students could help you find out. 3. You haven't planned how you'll know whether your solution worked. Consider counting the trays again after you try something and comparing the results.

Pitch Rubric
