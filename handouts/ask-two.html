Student learning · Grades K–2 · 35 minutes

Ask Two

A confident robot helper says things that are sometimes right and sometimes wrong, and children become learning owners by checking each answer in two places before they believe it.

Talking speakers, apps, and AI chatbots answer children's questions in a friendly, sure-sounding voice. Sounding sure is not the same as being right. In this lesson, Robo Helper (you, reading answer cards, plus an optional projected AI chatbot) gives answers to simple science and everyday questions. Pairs pick up a card and Ask Two: they check it at two of three stations (Book Nook, Look and See, Grown-Up Desk) before deciding right, wrong, or not sure yet. Children practice owning their learning: asking, checking, and changing their minds when the evidence says so.

In this packet

TCEA ELE indicators

  • S4.1 I know how to take responsibility for my own learning.
  • AI4.1 I know how to promote critical thinking skills in relation to AI-generated information.
  • S2.2 I can identify trustworthy sources of information on the internet.
  • S4.3 I am aware of my learning strengths and areas for improvement.
  • AI4.2 I can teach others to question and verify AI outputs.

My Ask Two Card

NameDate

Draw or write. Check in TWO places before you decide.

  1. Robo Helper said: ___

  2. Check 1: I looked in a book / used my eyes / asked a grown-up. I found:

  3. Check 2: I looked in a book / used my eyes / asked a grown-up. I found:

  4. Circle one: Robo was RIGHT · WRONG · NOT SURE YET

  5. I am good at checking by ___

  6. Next time I will ___
