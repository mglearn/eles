Student learning · Grades K–2 · 30 minutes

Robot Guesses

Children train a pretend robot with picture-word cards and discover that AI makes guesses from patterns, and that a guess can be wrong.

Young children already hear talking speakers and see apps that "know" things. In this game, the class becomes the teachers of Robo, a pretend robot that can only learn from the example cards it is shown and can only notice color. Robo makes confident guesses, gets some hilariously wrong, and gets better when the class gives it more kinds of examples. Children leave with a simple, true idea they can say out loud: "AI looks for patterns and makes guesses. Guesses can be wrong, so we check."

Handout C for Robot Guesses

My Robot Guess

NameDate

Draw and write. Your teacher can help you with words.

  1. Finish the pattern. Draw what comes next: star, circle, star, circle, star, ___

  2. Draw something Robo might guess WRONG.

  3. Robo guesses by looking for ___.

  4. When a robot or computer guesses, I can say: "Let's ___!"

  5. Draw a person who can help you check.

Facilitator only for Robot Guesses

Answer key and notes

Handout A: Pattern Cards

Card 1 · Sounds
stomp. Ask: "How did you know?"
Card 2 · Sizes
small. Children crouch down low.
Card 3 · Sky
moon. Children make a round moon with their arms.
Card 4 · Counting
2. The pattern has three parts that repeat.
Card 5 · Animals
cat. Two cats, then a dog, over and over.
Card 6 · Trick Card
Everyone guesses blue. Reveal: GREEN! The pattern changed. A smart guess can still be wrong.

Handout B: Robo's Training Cards

Training · Fruit
Goes under FRUIT. Robo learns: red means fruit.
Training · Fruit
Goes under FRUIT. Robo's pattern gets stronger: red, red.
Training · Fruit
Goes under FRUIT. Every fruit Robo has seen is red.
Training · Not a fruit
Goes under NOT A FRUIT. Robo learns: blue means not a fruit.
Training · Not a fruit
Goes under NOT A FRUIT. Robo learns: green means not a fruit.
Training · Not a fruit
Goes under NOT A FRUIT. Robo learns: yellow means not a fruit.
Test
Robo guesses NOT A FRUIT, because yellow things were never fruit in its examples. WRONG.
Test
Robo guesses FRUIT, because red things were always fruit. WRONG.
Test
Robo guesses FRUIT. RIGHT, but only because it happens to be red.
Test
Robo guesses NOT A FRUIT, because the frog was green. WRONG.
Fix-it · Fruit
Now Robo has seen a yellow fruit. Retest: Robo does better. More kinds of examples make better guesses.
Fix-it · Fruit
Robo is still only looking at color, so it may now mix up the green frog. Ask: "Is color a good way to tell what a fruit is?"
