The Glass Box Checklist

Teams interrogate a fictional AI vendor's glossy fact sheet, sort the questions that actually matter, and build a transparency and accountability checklist they can use on real tools.

AI tools arrive in schools with confident marketing: "safe," "unbiased," "research-based," "personalized." Transparency means being able to see behind those words: what the tool does, what data it uses, where it fails, and who answers for it when it's wrong. In this session teams read a fictional vendor fact sheet, sort a bank of evaluation questions, and build a short checklist that turns vague reassurance into answerable questions. Then they test the checklist on the published documentation of a tool their district already uses or is considering.

Handout A for The Glass Box Checklist

Mock Vendor Fact Sheet: BrightPath Writing Coach

FICTIONAL PRODUCT AND COMPANY, written for training. Highlight checkable claims in one color and vague claims in another.

1BrightPath Writing Coach: Every student, a confident writer. BrightPath uses advanced artificial intelligence to give every student instant, personalized feedback on their writing, 24 hours a day. Teachers save hours every week.

2How it works. Students paste or type their writing. Our proprietary AI engine analyzes it and returns feedback on organization, evidence, grammar, and style, plus a predicted rubric score. The feedback is generated by a large language model licensed from a third-party AI provider and customized with BrightPath's writing expertise.

3Research-based. BrightPath is grounded in the science of writing instruction and has been proven to accelerate student growth.

4Safe and fair. BrightPath is bias-free and appropriate for all learners, including English learners and students with disabilities. Our AI never makes mistakes that a teacher would not catch.

5Your data. Student writing is stored securely in the cloud. We may use de-identified student writing to improve our services and may share data with trusted partners. Accounts can be deleted by contacting support.

6Teacher control. Teachers can view all student feedback in the dashboard. Predicted scores can be synced automatically to your gradebook.

7Ages. Designed for grades 3–12. Setup takes under 5 minutes; students sign in with their school account.

Handout B for The Glass Box Checklist

Question Sort: cards to sort

Cut apart the strips. Sort each one onto the mat and be ready to explain why.

Sheet 1 of 2

What kind of AI produces the output, and does the vendor say which parts are generated versus rule-based?

What known limitations or failure cases does the vendor publish?

Has the tool been tested for how well it works for English learners, students with disabilities, and different dialects?

Does the tool tell students when content is AI-generated?

Is student work used to train or improve AI models, and can the district opt out?

Who are the "trusted partners," and what do they receive?

How long is student data kept, and is it deleted automatically when the contract ends?

Does the tool meet the district's data privacy agreement requirements for student records?

Who is responsible when the feedback or predicted score is wrong: the teacher, the district, or the vendor?

Can a teacher review and override every score before it reaches the gradebook?

How can a student or family question or appeal an AI-influenced decision?

How does the vendor notify schools of changes to the AI model or data practices?
