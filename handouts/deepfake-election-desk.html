Deepfake Election Desk

Student newsroom teams race a deadline to decide which election-week tips are real, which are synthetic, and which they simply can't confirm yet.

It is three days before the mayoral election in the fictional city of Port Alder, and tips are flooding the newsroom of the Port Alder Ledger: an audio clip, a rally photo, a video, a screenshot. Some are authentic, some are AI-generated or edited, and some cannot be confirmed before deadline. Student teams work as a verification desk, using provenance questions, lateral reading, and source contact rather than gut feel or a single detector, and they must write a publish, hold, or debunk decision they can defend. The big idea: with synthetic media, the most responsible answer is sometimes "we don't know yet," and saying so is part of the job.

Handout A for Deepfake Election Desk

Incoming Tips (Port Alder Mayoral Election)

Port Alder, the Ledger, both candidates, and every account are fictional. Flip one tip at a time. Print the backs (Reporting Results) separately; the teacher hands out a result only when a team asks the matching question.

Tip 1 · Audio clip

A 30-second audio clip is circulating in a messaging app. It sounds like candidate Dana Whitcomb saying, "Once I'm mayor, the east side bus routes are finished. Nobody out there votes for me anyway." It was forwarded to the newsroom by a reader who got it from a group chat.

Tip 2 · Rally photo

A photo shows an enormous crowd filling Harborview Park for candidate Luis Ortega's rally last night, with the caption "Port Alder has decided!" posted by @OrtegaForAlder, the campaign's official account.

Tip 3 · Flood photo

A photo of a flooded underpass with the caption "Whitcomb's failed drainage plan: Pier Street underpass this morning." It's been shared widely.

Tip 4 · Official notice

A screenshot of what looks like a text from the "Port Alder County Elections Office": "Due to high demand, Ortega voters vote Wednesday and Whitcomb voters vote Tuesday."

Tip 5 · Debate video

A video clip from last week's televised debate shows Luis Ortega misstating the city budget by a large amount. His supporters are calling it "an AI fake."

Tip 6 · Endorsement video

A video appears to show Port Alder's popular retired fire chief endorsing Whitcomb, standing in front of Station 3. It was posted by a local community page, not by either campaign.

Tip 7 · Translated post

A post in Spanish shared in a neighborhood group says Whitcomb promised to close the Linden Avenue community center. An auto-translated English version is spreading faster than the original.

Tip 8 · AI chatbot answer

A reader emails a screenshot: they asked an AI chatbot "When is the Port Alder mayoral election and where do I vote?" The answer gives a date one week late and an old polling location.

Facilitator only for Deepfake Election Desk

Answer key and notes

Handout A: Incoming Tips (Port Alder Mayoral Election)

Tip 1 · Audio clip
Origin: the earliest copy found was posted by a brand-new account with no other posts. Contact: the Whitcomb campaign says she never said it and provides her full schedule for the claimed date. Corroboration: no event, recording, or witness matches. The voice's pacing is unusually even. Status: Don't amplify / Hold; if it spreads widely, publish a debunk that doesn't replay the audio.
Tip 2 · Rally photo
Context: people who attended describe a crowd of roughly two hundred. A reporter's own photos show the park's front lawn only. Origin: the campaign's image has repeating faces in the back rows and a banner with warped lettering. Contact: the campaign says a volunteer "enhanced" the image. Status: Publish a story on the campaign's altered image, using your reporter's authentic photo.
Tip 3 · Flood photo
Context: a reverse image search finds the same photo in a news story from a different city six years ago. Contact: Port Alder Public Works says Pier Street had no flooding this week. Status: Real photo, false context. Publish a debunk.
Tip 4 · Official notice
Contact: the county elections office confirms election day is the same for every voter and it sends no texts about voting days. Origin: the sending number is not the county's. Status: Publish a debunk immediately with the correct information. This is voter suppression, the highest-harm tip on the desk.
Tip 5 · Debate video
Origin: the full debate broadcast is available from the local station, and the moment appears at the same spot. Corroboration: two reporters in the room took notes on the same statement. Contact: the Ortega campaign later issued a correction. Status: Authentic. Publish with the correction included. Claiming real evidence is fake is its own form of misinformation.
Tip 6 · Endorsement video
Contact: the retired chief's family says he is traveling and unreachable until after the election. The community page admin says a follower sent it. Corroboration: Station 3 confirms the background matches its building, but the uniform shown was retired years ago. Status: Hold. Too many unknowns. Report only that you're working to verify it.
Tip 7 · Translated post
Origin: a bilingual reporter reads the original. It says Whitcomb wants to review the center's hours, not close it; the auto-translation turned "revisar" into "shut down." Contact: the campaign confirms "review." Status: Publish a correction in both languages. Translation errors, human or machine, can create false claims.
Tip 8 · AI chatbot answer
Corroboration: the county elections website lists the correct date and current polling places. The chatbot's answer matches an older election. Status: Publish a service note telling readers to confirm voting information directly with the county elections office, not with a chatbot.
