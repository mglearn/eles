Algorithm Autopsy

Students dissect the design features of a fictional social app, trace how each one shapes attention and belief, and redesign the feed for people instead of engagement.

Recommendation systems are a form of AI most students use every day without choosing to. They predict what will keep a person watching and rank content accordingly, and the app around them is designed with features like autoplay, streaks, and infinite scroll that keep people on the app longer. In this autopsy, teams examine the features of a fictional app called Loop, sort each one by what it is optimized to do, and discuss in a Socratic seminar whether a feed can shape what people believe. They finish by designing a healthier feed with specific rules, trade-offs, and a way to measure whether it works.

Handout A for Algorithm Autopsy

Loop Feature Autopsy: cards to sort

Cut apart the strips. Sort each one onto the mat and be ready to explain why.

Sheet 1 of 2

1. Infinite scroll: the feed never ends; there's no bottom of the page.

2. Autoplay: the next video starts automatically after three seconds.

3. "For You" ranking: the main feed is ranked by predicted watch time, not by who you follow or when it was posted.

4. Streaks: a counter shows how many days in a row you've posted or messaged a friend.

5. Trending topics: a sidebar lists the topics getting the most reactions right now.

6. "Not interested" button: tap it and the feed shows less of that kind of content.

7. Push notifications: "3 friends posted while you were away!"

8. Following-only view: a setting to see only accounts you follow, in time order.

9. Engagement boost: posts with many comments and shares get shown to more people, whether the reactions are positive or angry.

10. Like counts: every post shows its number of likes.

11. Daily time limit: users can set a reminder after a chosen number of minutes.

12. "Why am I seeing this?" panel: explains that a post appeared because you watched similar videos.

Handout B for Algorithm Autopsy

The Loop Memo (Seminar Text)

This memo is fictional. Annotate: underline claims, circle words that sound neutral but aren't, and write one question in the margin for the seminar.

1LOOP INTERNAL MEMO · Product Team · Subject: Q3 Feed Update

2Team, great news. Our new ranking model, which predicts how long each user will watch each video, has increased average daily time on Loop. Users are clearly finding more of what they love.

3The model learns from every signal: watch time, rewatches, pauses, comments, shares, and how fast someone scrolls past. We've found that videos generating strong emotional reactions, whether excitement, surprise, or anger, keep people watching longest, so the model naturally shows more of them.

4Some team members raised a concern that users who watch one conspiracy-style video are now seeing many more. We want to be clear: Loop doesn't choose what people believe. We simply show people what they want. If someone watches it, they must want it.

5We also tested a "Following-only" default. Time on the app went down, so we've kept it as an optional setting, available three menus deep.

6Next quarter, we'll add streaks to direct messages and move the daily time limit reminder to after 90 minutes instead of 30, since users told us it was annoying.

7Our mission remains the same: connect people with the content that matters to them.
