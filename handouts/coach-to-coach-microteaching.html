Coach-to-Coach Microteaching

Coaches plan and deliver 10-minute micro-PD sessions on one digital literacy move, get protocol-based peer feedback, and revise before a teacher ever sees it.

Coaches are often asked to teach a skill to adults in the ten minutes at the start of a staff meeting, and those ten minutes rarely get rehearsed. In this session, each coach draws one digital literacy move (tracing a quote, checking an AI answer's sources, a privacy pause before uploading), plans a 10-minute micro-PD with a RISE goal and adult learning principles built in, delivers it to two colleagues, and receives feedback through a structured glow, grow, and try protocol. Coaches also test an AI-drafted set of feedback stems and decide what to keep. Everyone revises and re-delivers the weakest two minutes before leaving.

Handout A for Coach-to-Coach Microteaching

Move Cards and AI-Drafted Stems

Cut apart. Each coach draws one Move card. The two AI-Drafted Stems cards are for the whole trio. Backs are the key idea and the common misconception.

Sheet 1 of 2

Move · Trace the quote

Before sharing a quotation from a post, find where the person actually said it and read the sentences around it.

Move · Check the AI's sources

When a chatbot cites a source, open it. Does it exist? Does it say what the chatbot claims?

Move · Who's behind this site?

Leave the page and search the organization's name to learn who runs and funds it before trusting its claims.

Move · Find the original image

Use a reverse image search, or look for the earliest post, to see where and when a photo first appeared.

Move · Check the date

Before reacting to a story, look for when it was published and whether it's being recirculated as new.

Move · The privacy pause

Before uploading anything to an app or AI tool, ask: whose information is in this, and where does it go?

Move · Disclose the AI help

Add a short note to work that AI helped with: which tool, what it did, what you changed and checked.

Move · Name the persuasion

Before sharing, name the technique the post uses: urgency, fear, flattery, bandwagon, or a strong emotion.

Answer key and notes

Handout A: Move Cards and AI-Drafted Stems

Move · Trace the quote
Why it matters: quotations are often clipped to reverse their meaning. Misconception: "If there's a name attached, it's real."
Move · Check the AI's sources
Why it matters: chatbots can produce plausible citations that don't exist or don't support the claim. Misconception: "It gave a source, so it's checked."
Move · Who's behind this site?
Why it matters: a professional look and a .org address tell you nothing about bias. Misconception: "I'll read the About page."
Move · Find the original image
Why it matters: real photos are often reshared with false context. Misconception: "I can tell by looking whether it's edited."
Move · Check the date
Why it matters: old stories resurface as breaking news. Misconception: "If it's in my feed today, it happened today."
Move · The privacy pause
Why it matters: student names and work end up in tools that keep or train on them. Misconception: "It's a school device, so it's safe."
Move · Disclose the AI help
Why it matters: disclosure builds trust and makes the student's own thinking visible. Misconception: "Disclosure means admitting you cheated."
Move · Name the persuasion
Why it matters: naming the technique slows the share. Misconception: "Only other people fall for that."
AI-Drafted Stems · Glows
Keep 3 (it names a rubric criterion). Revise 2 to cite a moment. Cut 1 and 4: generic praise, and 4 counts tool use as quality.
AI-Drafted Stems · Grows
Keep 3 and 4 (specific, tied to the rubric). Revise 1 to name which segment. Cut 2: it evaluates the person, not the session.
