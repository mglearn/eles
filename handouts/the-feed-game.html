The Feed Game

Students play a card-based recommendation engine by hand and watch their feed shrink to a few topics, then design ways to take it back.

Recommendation systems don't know what is true, kind, or good for you. They predict what will keep you watching, based on patterns in what you did before. In this game, one student plays the Algorithm and follows a printed rule card to choose posts for a Viewer, scoring every reaction. In five rounds, most feeds narrow to one or two topics, and posts designed to spark strong feelings rise to the top. Students then change one rule and discover what they can and can't control.

In this packet

  • Handout A: Feed Cards (cut-apart cards)
  • Handout B: Feed Tracker (organizer)

  • AI1.1 I know how AI systems work and their capabilities in educational contexts.

  • S2.2 I can identify trustworthy sources of information on the internet.

Handout A for The Feed Game

Feed Cards

Cut apart. Give the two Rule Cards to the Algorithm. Shuffle the 14 post cards face down. Posts marked Spark earn +1 point when a Viewer watches, likes, or shares them.

Sheet 1 of 2

Rule Card 1 · Scoring

For every post the Viewer sees, score the reaction: Skip = 0, Watch all the way = 1, Like = 2, Comment or Share = 3. Spark post (and not skipped) = +1 bonus. Add the points to that post's topic. You never check if a post is true, kind, or helpful. You only count reactions.

Rule Card 2 · Next round

Round 1: deal 4 random posts. Every round after: deal 3 posts from the topic with the most total points and 1 post from the topic in second place. Tie? Pick the topic with more Spark points. Topic out of cards? Reshuffle its seen cards back in. Feeds repeat.

Handout C for The Feed Game

Take Back Your Feed

NameDate

Answer on your own using your team's Feed Tracker.

  1. In Round 1, our feed showed ___ topics. In Round 5, it showed ___ topics. Explain why, using the rule cards.

  2. Why did Spark posts keep getting boosted? What does that mean for posts that are angry, shocking, or untrue?

  3. The Algorithm knew only our reactions. List two things it did NOT know about the Viewer.

  4. Our rule change was… Did the feed widen? What was the trade-off?

  5. One habit I'll try this week to shape my feed on purpose (for example: skip posts that only exist to make me mad, look up a topic I want to learn about, stop sharing before I check):
