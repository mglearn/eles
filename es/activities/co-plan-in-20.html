Co-Plan in 20

A timed, listening-first protocol a coach and teacher use to redesign one upcoming lesson so students verify an AI answer or evaluate a source, and turn in proof that they did.

Resumen

Most teachers have twenty minutes, not an afternoon, and most coaching conversations drift toward a tool before anyone has named the learning. Co-Plan in 20 sits in the Investigate and Innovate phase of the TCEA EIIR coaching cycle: a five-segment protocol that fits a planning period. The coach listens, uses the ARC moves (Acknowledge the reality, Reframe the problem from a student deficit to a design barrier, Commit to one high-leverage shift), redesigns one moment of the lesson, models it, and sets a date to look at student work. Participants run it in pairs on a real or persona lesson where students use AI output or online sources, then debrief what kept the conversation on the teacher's goal.

Objetivos

  • Participants will use listening-first stems for the opening four minutes and paraphrase the teacher's goal ("What I hear you saying is…") before offering any idea.
  • Participants will reframe the teacher's concern from something wrong with students to something the lesson design can change, then co-plan one shift that requires students to verify an AI answer or evaluate a source.
  • Participants will keep the learning goal firm while offering flexible ways for students to show the check, and name the student artifact that will prove it happened.
  • Participants will model the verification move in a 60-second student-facing think-aloud and agree on a date to review student work together.

Materiales

En papel

  • Handout A: Role Cards (1 set per pair, cut apart)
  • Handout B: The Listening-First Script (1 per coach)
  • Handout C: Co-Planning Template (1 per pair)
  • A visible timer; a bell or chime for segment changes

En pantalla

  • Optional: a shared copy of Handout C that the coach and teacher both keep
  • Optional: a timer on the projector showing the five segments

Antes de empezar

  1. Print and cut Handout A. Keep the six Teacher Persona cards together so pairs can choose one, and give each pair one Coach card and one Observer card.
  2. Invite teachers in the room to bring a real lesson they will teach in the next two weeks. A real lesson always beats a persona card.
  3. Practice the 60-second think-aloud in Segment 4 on one persona card so you can model it without notes.

Paso a paso

  1. 10–5 min

    Enganche

    Where did your last planning conversation go?

    Ask coaches to recall their last planning conversation with a teacher and answer silently: "How many minutes passed before someone named a tool or an app?" Then ask: "How many minutes passed before someone named what students would turn in?" Take two or three answers. Say: "Today's protocol flips that order. We name the evidence first and decide about the tool, or no tool, last."

    Nota para el facilitadorMost people admit the tool came first. Keep it light; the point is noticing the habit, not judging it.

  2. 25–10 min

    Explorar

    Read the protocol

    Everyone reads Handout B. Point out the five segments and their clock times: Listen and Acknowledge (0–4), Reframe and name the evidence (4–8), Commit to one shift (8–15), Model it (15–18), Plan the measure (18–20). The middle three carry the ARC protocol. Ask coaches to circle the one stem they rarely use and underline the segment they expect to be hardest.

    Nota para el facilitadorCoaches usually underline Reframe. "They believe everything it says" is a statement about students; "The lesson never gives them a moment to check" is a statement about design, and only the second one gives the teacher something to change.

  3. 310–18 min

    Modelar

    Fishbowl the first eight minutes

    Invite a volunteer to play Teacher Persona 3 (Grade 8 ELA). You play the coach in front of the room for Segments 1 and 2 only. Use only listening stems for four minutes, then acknowledge: "What I hear you saying is that your students pick sources that agree with them, and it's frustrating because you've taught them what bias is. Did I get that right?" Then reframe out loud: "What if the problem isn't that they can't see bias, but that the assignment only ever asks them to find support? Where in the lesson are they asked to find the other side?" Finally ask the evidence question: "If this worked, what would you see on their paper?" Stop at the eight-minute chime and ask the room: "What did I not do?"

    Nota para el facilitadorThe answer you want: you did not suggest a fix, a tool, or a strategy. Name that restraint as a move, not a pause.

  4. 418–38 min

    Practicar

    Run the full protocol

    Pairs choose a real lesson or a Teacher Persona card from Handout A. The coach uses Handout B; the teacher holds the persona card and reveals the hidden detail only if the coach asks a question that earns it. Ring the chime at 4, 8, 15, and 18 minutes. Pairs fill in Handout C as they go. In Segment 2 the coach offers a reframe the teacher can accept or reject. In Segment 3 they commit to one shift (trace a claim, compare an AI answer to a source, read laterally about a website, or check a date), hold the goal firm, and list at least two ways students could show the check (a written note, a sorted card, a sentence stem said to a partner, a sketch). They also decide together whether AI belongs in the lesson at all. In Segment 4 the coach models the move as a 60-second think-aloud aimed at the teacher's students. In Segment 5 they write the artifact, the date, and the question they will ask of the student work.

    Nota para el facilitadorIf there is a third person, they take the Observer card and tally coach questions and coach suggestions in each segment. Watch for pairs that redesign the whole unit. One shift is the assignment; ARC stands for one commitment, not a list.

  5. 538–45 min

    Análisis

    What kept it on the teacher's goal?

    Pairs report in one sentence each: "The teacher's goal was… The student evidence we'll collect is… We decided AI does / does not belong because…" Then ask the teacher-players: "When did you feel heard? When did you feel steered?" Chart the moves they name.

    Nota para el facilitadorTeacher-players often say the paraphrase was the moment they felt heard, and a suggestion in the first minutes was when they felt steered. That is C1.2 feedback running both ways.

  6. 645–50 min

    Transferir

    Book the real twenty minutes

    Coaches write the name of one teacher and a date on the back of Handout C and the first stem they will use. Teachers who brought a real lesson keep their completed Handout C and agree to send the coach three pieces of student work after teaching it.

    Nota para el facilitadorSuggest coaches offer the protocol by name: "Could I have twenty minutes of your planning period? We'll leave with one change and a date." A fixed time limit lowers the barrier, and showing up on the date you promised is what builds trust for the next conversation.

Papel o pantalla

Sin pantallas

The protocol is designed for a table, a timer, and paper. The coach reads stems from Handout B, the pair fills in Handout C by hand, and the Segment 4 model is a spoken think-aloud using a printed source or an AI answer the teacher already has. If the lesson involves an AI answer, the teacher can bring a printed copy of one.

Digital

In a virtual session, send pairs to breakout rooms with a shared copy of Handout C and a countdown timer posted in the chat. In a real coaching conversation, keeping Handout C in a shared document gives the coach and teacher one record to reopen when the student work comes back. If the lesson uses an AI chatbot the district approves, the teacher can drive it on a projector in Segment 4 so the model uses a live answer.

¿Necesita una pantalla? Unplugged is the better default: the skill is listening, and a laptop between coach and teacher pulls attention away. A shared planning document earns its place afterward, when the student work comes back and both of you need to see what was planned and what changed.

Evidencia de aprendizaje

Lo que debería poder ver o recopilar si funcionó.

  • Observer tallies show questions, not suggestions, in Segment 1, and the teacher agrees with the coach's paraphrase before Segment 3 begins.
  • Handout C records a reframe that moves the problem from the students to the lesson design, and one committed shift rather than a list.
  • Handout C names one specific student artifact, at least two ways students could show it, and a date to review it.
  • The pair records a reason for its AI decision, including "AI does not belong in this lesson" when that is the honest answer.

Adaptaciones

K–2 teachers
The verification moment is teacher-led: the teacher drives any AI tool on a projector, and students vote thumbs up or down on whether a picture book agrees with the chatbot. The artifact is a class chart, not individual work.
Higher Ed faculty
Use Persona 6 or a real syllabus assignment. The artifact might be a short source note students attach to a discussion post naming how they checked one claim.
Librarians
Take the coach role with a content teacher and bring two short sources to Segment 4 so the model uses real texts from the collection.
New coaches
Run Segments 1 and 2 twice with different personas before running the full twenty minutes. Listening is the segment most likely to collapse.

Reflexionar

  • How effectively am I supporting teachers in improving their instructional practices? (Coach ELE guiding question)
  • In what ways am I helping teachers meaningfully integrate technology, and when did I help a teacher decide not to use it?
  • What individual need did this teacher reveal in the first four minutes that I would have missed if I had started with a suggestion?

Llévelo a sus estudiantes

Run Co-Plan in 20 with the teacher you named this week. When the three pieces of student work come back, spend ten minutes together answering the question you wrote in Segment 5, then decide the next adjustment.
