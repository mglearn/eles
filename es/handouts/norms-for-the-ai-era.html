Norms for the AI Era

A PLC builds trust first, then sorts real shared-planning dilemmas and drafts its own norms for AI use: disclose it, check it, and never put student data in a prompt.

Shared planning folders now hold quizzes, rubrics, and parent letters that a chatbot helped draft, and teams rarely talk about it. One teammate feels uneasy using an unmarked AI-written quiz; another quietly pasted student writing into a tool to get feedback ideas. This session gives a PLC a structured way to talk about it before it becomes a conflict: a Hopes, Hesitations, and Needs round built on three kinds of trust, a sort of realistic dilemmas, and a one-page set of team norms on disclosure, verification, and privacy that the team agrees to revisit.

En este paquete

  • Hoja A: Trust Protocol Cards (tarjetas para recortar)
  • Hoja B: Shared Planning Dilemmas Sort (clasificación de tarjetas)

Hoja A de Norms for the AI Era

Trust Protocol Cards

Cut apart. The coach reads the three Trust Type cards in the Hook; the team uses the Round and Rule cards in the protocol. Backs are facilitator notes.

Página 1 de 2

Trust Type · Communicative

Trust built by what we say and how we follow through on it: listening well, paraphrasing, being clear about expectations, and keeping shared notes so no one is surprised.

Trust Type · Relational

Trust built by how we treat each other: noticing and celebrating wins, being present and dependable, and showing empathy when something is hard.

Trust Type · Competence-based

Trust built by doing what we said we would do, well: following through promptly on promised help, anticipating needs, and grounding advice in sound practice.

Round 1 · Hopes

"One way AI could help our team plan better is…"

Round 2 · Hesitations

"One thing that worries me about AI in shared planning is…"

Round 3 · Needs

"To trust a resource a teammate shares, I need to know…"

Rule · Paraphrase first

Before you add your answer, restate the person before you: "What I hear you saying is…" Check: "Did I get that right?" Then add yours.

Rule · Equal voice

Speak only with the talking piece. Everyone speaks once per round. You may pass once. The coach speaks last.

Hoja C de Norms for the AI Era

Our Team's AI Norms

NombreFecha

Write each norm as an action a visitor could see. Test the draft against the two cases at the bottom.

  1. Disclosure: When AI helps make something we share, we…

  2. Verification: Before anything AI-drafted reaches students or families, we…

  3. Privacy: We never put these into a prompt… (list them), and when in doubt we…

  4. Trust: When someone is uncomfortable with a shared resource or with how AI was used, they can…, and we will…

  5. Revisit: We will check these norms against real files in our shared folder on (date), and the person who brings three examples is…

  6. Test case 1: A teammate used AI to translate a letter about the field trip into Arabic. A parent calls to say one sentence is confusing. Do our norms say what to do? What would we change?

  7. Test case 2: A teammate says, "I don't want to use any AI-drafted material with my students." Do our norms respect that and still let the team plan together? What would we change?

Clave de respuestas y notas

Hoja A: Trust Protocol Cards

Trust Type · Communicative
In this session: the paraphrase rule and the written norms.
Trust Type · Relational
In this session: the closing win round, and taking hesitations seriously instead of arguing with them.
Trust Type · Competence-based
In this session: the coach's private follow-up list, delivered within a week.
Round 1 · Hopes
Hopes first lowers the temperature and reminds skeptics that the team is not choosing sides.
Round 2 · Hesitations
Do not answer or correct hesitations during the round. Record them. They become norms.
Round 3 · Needs
Typical answers: who made it, whether anyone checked it, whether student information went into it. Each one is a norm.
Rule · Paraphrase first
Slows the conversation just enough that people feel heard. Model it yourself if the first speaker skips it.
Rule · Equal voice
The coach speaking last keeps the team's voice ahead of the coach's.
Closing · A win
Keep it specific and brief. It ends the session on shared purpose.

Asking a chatbot to summarize last week's PLC notes, which include comments about two named students' behavior.Stop: remove student or colleague data firstPrivacy: the notes identify students. Summarize by hand or remove names and details first.
Using AI to translate a newsletter to families into Spanish and Vietnamese.Pause: ask the team or districtAccuracy and access: families deserve an accurate translation; check whether the district has translation guidance or a human reviewer.
Generating a rubric with AI and adapting it to the team's writing standards.Go ahead and discloseTransparency: note that AI drafted it and the team revised it.
Uploading the whole class roster with reading scores to an AI tool to form small groups.Stop: remove student or colleague data firstPrivacy: never upload identifiable student data to a tool that is not district-approved for it.
A teammate uses a new AI lesson-plan app the district hasn't reviewed and wants to share its plans.Pause: ask the team or districtPrivacy and accuracy: unreviewed tools raise data and quality questions the team can't settle alone.
Asking a chatbot for five common misconceptions about fractions, then checking them against the curriculum guide.Go aheadAccuracy is handled by the check against a trusted source.
Creating an AI-generated image of a historical figure for a slide students will see.Go ahead and discloseTransparency and media literacy: label it as generated so students don't mistake it for a historical image.
