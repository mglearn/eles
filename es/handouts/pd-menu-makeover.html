PD Menu Makeover

Coaches take apart a one-size-fits-all AI PD day and rebuild it as a choice menu, grounded in a real needs assessment, adult learning principles, and teaching strategies that already work.

Many campuses have lived through the AI PD day: a keynote about the future, a demo of eight tools, everyone creates an account, and nothing changes on Monday. In this design session, coaches analyze a fictional district's original agenda and the comments teachers left afterward, sort a draft needs assessment into questions worth keeping, rewriting, or cutting, and then rebuild the day as a menu. Every menu option has to name who it is for, the adult learning principle it honors, the research-based teaching strategy it serves, and the evidence teachers will bring back. The menu includes paths for skeptics, for privacy, and for deciding not to use AI.

En este paquete

  • Hoja A: The Juniper Flats AI Day (lectura)
  • Hoja B: Needs Assessment Sort (clasificación de tarjetas)
  • Hoja C: Menu Builder (organizador)
  • Clave del facilitador (última página)

  • C4.3 Conozco los principios del aprendizaje de adultos y cómo aplicarlos.
  • AI1.2 Puedo explicar los conceptos de IA en términos sencillos a diversas partes interesadas.

Hoja A de PD Menu Makeover

The Juniper Flats AI Day

A fictional district's original PD agenda and the comments teachers wrote afterward. Starred comments are for the Persona check.

1Juniper Flats ISD · "AI for Everyone" PD Day · All K–12 staff, high school auditorium. 8:00 Welcome and keynote: "The Future Is Already Here." · 9:15 Demo: eight AI tools in 75 minutes. · 10:30 Everyone creates an account on one chatbot and tries three prompts from the slide. · 11:30 Lunch. · 12:30 Compliance video on acceptable use. · 1:30 Grade-level teams "brainstorm ways to use AI." · 2:45 Exit survey: "Rate today 1–5."

2Six adult learning principles to diagnose with. (1) Adults need to know why before what. (2) Adults bring experience that should be used, not ignored. (3) Adults learn best around real problems in their own work. (4) Adults want choice and some control over their learning. (5) Adults are ready to learn what is relevant to their role right now. (6) Adults need practice with feedback, not just exposure.

3★ Grade 2 teacher: "Seven of the eight tools said 13 and up. I teach seven-year-olds. I spent the morning on things I can't use."

4★ High school chemistry teacher: "I'm not against AI, I'm against wasting a day. I wanted to know whether it can help my students write a claim with evidence in a lab report. Nobody asked me that."

5★ Middle school ELA teacher, 22 years in: "I have real concerns about my students outsourcing their thinking, and today felt like a sales pitch. I didn't say anything because it seemed like the answer was already yes."

6Special education teacher: "I would have loved a session on text-to-speech and reading supports. It was mentioned for thirty seconds."

7Librarian: "We created accounts with our district email, and nobody explained what the tool does with what we type. Two people at my table pasted in student writing to test it."

8Second-year teacher: "Honestly, I just want to see someone teach a lesson with it and then try it myself with help nearby."

9Exit survey note from the organizers: "Average rating 3.1. Teachers want more time to explore." (Ask yourself whether that conclusion matches the comments above.)

Needs Assessment Sort: tarjetas para clasificar

Recorta las tiras. Coloca cada una en el tapete de clasificación y prepárate para explicar por qué.

Which AI tools would you like to learn about?

What is one thing you want students to be able to do by winter break that they struggle with now?

Rate your comfort with technology from 1 to 5.

What concerns, if any, do you have about AI in your classroom? (Your answer will not be shared with your name.)

Do you agree that AI will transform education?

How do you prefer to learn something new: watching a model, trying it with support, reading, listening, or discussing with colleagues?

List all the apps you use in a typical week.

What do you already do well that you could share with colleagues?

Have you ever used AI to write lesson plans? Yes / No

What grade level and subject do you teach?

Please enter your students' names and reading levels so we can personalize the session.

How much time can you realistically give to follow-up after the PD day?

Hoja B: Needs Assessment Sort

ElementoCategoríaPor qué
Which AI tools would you like to learn about?RewriteAsks about tools, not needs. Better: "What do you want students to do better this semester?"
What is one thing you want students to be able to do by winter break that they struggle with now?KeepSurfaces a classroom goal the PD can serve.
Rate your comfort with technology from 1 to 5.RewriteToo vague to act on. Better: ask about comfort with a specific task, such as checking an AI answer against a source.
What concerns, if any, do you have about AI in your classroom? (Your answer will not be shared with your name.)KeepGives skeptics a safe, anonymous way to be heard.
Do you agree that AI will transform education?CutA leading question that signals the expected answer and yields no planning information.
How do you prefer to learn something new: watching a model, trying it with support, reading, listening, or discussing with colleagues?KeepDirectly informs format choices on the menu.
List all the apps you use in a typical week.CutLong, tedious, and says little about learning needs.
What do you already do well that you could share with colleagues?KeepHonors experience and finds peer facilitators.
Have you ever used AI to write lesson plans? Yes / NoRewriteA yes or no can feel like a trap. Better: "Where, if anywhere, has AI saved you time or caused a problem?"
What grade level and subject do you teach?KeepNeeded so K–2 teachers are not sent to tools for 13 and up.
