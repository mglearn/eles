Your Face, Your Say

Middle schoolers learn that your face and body are yours, that using AI to make a fake, hurtful image of a real person is never a joke and can be against the law, and exactly what to do if they see one, tell a trusted adult and don't pass it on.

Middle schoolers are meeting apps that can paste anyone's face into fake images or change a real photo into something embarrassing or cruel. This lesson gives them a clear, age-appropriate foundation before they face it: your likeness, your face and body, belongs to you, and no one has the right to make or share a fake, hurtful image of you or anyone else. Without any explicit content, students learn that using AI to make a non-consensual, fake sexualized or humiliating image of a real person (a non-consensual image made without their permission) is not a prank, hurts a real person, and can be against the law, even for kids. The heart of the lesson is what to do: if you're targeted, it's not your fault and you tell a trusted adult; if you see one of someone else, you don't laugh, don't share it, tell an adult, and check on the person. Students practice being the upstander who refuses to pass it on. The tone is calm, respectful, and empowering, not scary, centered on consent, dignity, and looking out for each other.

Hoja B de Your Face, Your Say

What Would You Do?

You're the bystander. For each card, pick the UPSTANDER moves (don't share, don't laugh, tell a trusted adult, check on the person) and reject the spread-it moves. Practice one real thing to say or do.

the chat

A fake, embarrassing image of a classmate pops up in a group chat and people start laughing.

the friend

Your friend forwards you a fake image of someone and says 'send it to everyone.'

the dare

Someone dares you to make a fake image of a classmate with an app 'as a joke.'

the target

A classmate tells you a fake image of them is going around and they're scared.

the bystander guilt

You already laughed or shared before you realized how bad it was. What now?

practice

Say out loud one line you could actually use to refuse to pass something on in a group chat.

Clave de respuestas y notas

Hoja A: Whose Image Is It?

ElementoCategoríaPor qué
Drawing a silly cartoon of yourself and posting itOkayIt's you, and you chose to share it.
Using an app to put a classmate's face on a fake, embarrassing body and sending it aroundNot okayNo consent, and it hurts a real person. This can be abuse and illegal.
Posting a group photo after everyone in it said yesOkayEveryone consented to the picture being shared.
Editing a friend's selfie to make them look ridiculous and posting it without askingNot okayIt's their image and they didn't agree; it's meant to humiliate.
Making a fake sexual image of a real person with an appNot okayThis is image-based sexual abuse. It's never a joke and can be a crime, even for minors.
Making a meme using a cartoon character (not a real person)OkayNo real person's likeness is being misused.
Forwarding a fake, cruel image of a classmate because it's 'funny'Not okayForwarding spreads the harm; you become part of it.
Asking a friend 'can I post this picture of you?' and only posting if they say yesOkayThat's consent, and respect for their image.
Using someone's photo to make them look like they did something they didn'tNot okayIt's a lie that uses their likeness and can seriously hurt them.
Telling a trusted adult when you see a fake, hurtful image of someoneOkayThat's the upstander move, it helps stop the harm.

Hoja B: What Would You Do?

the chat
Upstander: don't react or forward, leave or say "not okay," screenshot only if an adult asks, and tell a trusted adult. Check on the classmate privately.
the friend
Upstander: "No, that's messed up and it can get us both in real trouble, I'm not sharing it." Tell an adult.
the dare
Upstander: say no, it's not a joke, it's abuse and illegal. Don't do it, and tell an adult if it's really happening.
the target
Upstander: "It's not your fault. Let's tell [trusted adult] right now, they can help get it taken down." Stay with them.
the bystander guilt
It's not too late: stop sharing, tell an adult what happened, and help make it right. Owning it is the strong move.
practice
Examples: "Not sharing that." "That's not funny, it's someone's life." "I'm out." Short and clear works.
