Create or Just Scroll?

A student and a family member log one real day of screen use and tag each piece as making something or just taking it in, discovering that what you do on a screen often matters more than how many minutes it takes.

When families talk about screen use, they usually count minutes. This activity adds a better question: what were you doing? Students learn to tag screen moments as creating (making a video, a drawing, a story, a message to someone) or consuming (watching, scrolling, listening, playing someone else's game). Both are okay, and consuming is not evil, but researchers increasingly find that the kind of screen use often matters more than the raw amount. Students practice tagging in class, then log a real day at home with a family member, who tracks their own day too. Together they look at the balance of create versus consume, notice when consuming turned into autopilot scrolling, and pick one small way to shift the mix, not to use screens less, but to use more of their screen time making things. The stance stays balanced: this is about noticing and choosing, not guilt about screen time.

Phiếu B cho Create or Just Scroll?

Our One-Day Screen Log

Họ tênNgày

One log for you, one for a family member. For one normal day, write each screen moment. Mark C for Create or T for Take-in (consume), and rough minutes. Be honest, no cleanup! At the end of the day, color each row and add up your mix.

My Mix and My Move

  3. How did my family member's mix compare to mine? What did we notice together?
