The Dinner Table Deal

Young children notice how it feels when a screen interrupts time together, act it out, and make a simple "table deal" with their family about one screen-free time, so everyone, grown-ups too, gets a turn to be listened to.

Little kids feel it when a grown-up's eyes slide to a phone in the middle of their story, and grown-ups feel it when a child won't look up from a tablet at dinner. This lesson gives young children gentle words for that feeling and a tiny, fair plan to fix it. Children act out two short scenes, one where a screen interrupts time together and one where it doesn't, and notice the difference in their bodies and feelings. They learn that this is not about screens being bad; screens help us a lot, and there is a right time for them. Then they take home a "table deal": one screen-free time the whole family picks together, where everyone, kids and grown-ups, puts devices away so they can really listen to each other. The deal is on purpose a two-way promise, because children learn balance best when the adults they love practice it too.

Phiếu A cho The Dinner Table Deal

Two Scenes

Act these out with a pretend phone (a block or card). After each scene, talk about how it felt.

scene 1

Dinner time. One person keeps looking at a phone. The others try to tell about their day, but the phone keeps pulling the eyes away.

scene 2

Same dinner. The phone is resting in a basket. Everyone looks at each other and listens. One person shares good news and everyone hears it.

try it

A child wants to show a grown-up a picture they drew, but the grown-up is scrolling. Act out a kind way to ask for listening.

try it

A grown-up wants to hear about a child's day, but the child is deep in a tablet game. Act out a kind way to ask for listening.

both ways

Show a moment where a screen HELPED the family (a video call with a far-away grandparent, a recipe, a song to dance to).

both ways

Show the family choosing their listening time together and everyone putting phones in the basket, grown-ups too.

Phiếu C cho The Dinner Table Deal

Our Family Table Deal

Họ tênNgày

Start at school. Finish at home with your family. Family note: this is not about screens being bad, they help us a lot. It is a two-way promise to protect one listening time where EVERYONE, grown-ups too, puts devices away. Please make this deal together with your child.

  1. Draw the one time our family will make screen-free so we can listen to each other (dinner, bedtime, the car, breakfast).

  2. Draw where the phones will wait during our listening time (a basket, a shelf, another room).

  3. The kind words we will use: "I want to hear you, so I'm putting my phone away."

Đáp án và ghi chú

Phiếu A: Two Scenes

scene 1
Ask: How did it feel for the people trying to share? For the person on the phone? (Let a grown-up be the one on the phone sometimes.)
scene 2
Ask: How did this feel different? What did your body do? What did your feelings do?
try it
Practice: "Can we have phones away so I can show you something?" The grown-up says, "Yes, I'm listening," and puts the phone down.
try it
Practice: "I want to hear about your day. Let's put screens away for a little." Everyone gets a turn to ask and to listen.
both ways
Remember: screens are good and help us a lot. The deal is only about one listening time, not about screens being bad.
both ways
The deal is for EVERYBODY. That is what makes it fair and easy to keep.
