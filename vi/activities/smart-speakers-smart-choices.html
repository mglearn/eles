Smart Speakers, Smart Choices

Children role-play with a pretend talking speaker to learn that voice helpers listen for a wake word, may keep what they hear, answer by guessing, and sometimes need a grown-up instead.

Phiếu

  • A Pip's Script Cards (thẻ cắt rời)

In phiếu

Tổng quan

Many children talk to smart speakers, talking toys, and voice assistants on phones or tablets at home, and they often treat them like a friend who knows everything. In this role-play, a classmate wearing a paper "speaker" becomes Pip, a pretend voice helper. The class discovers three true things: Pip waits for its wake word and then listens closely, Pip may save what it hears on a far-away computer, and Pip answers by guessing from patterns, so it can be wrong. Children practice sorting what is OK to say to a device, what to keep private, and when to ask a grown-up instead.

Mục tiêu

  • Students will explain in their own words that a voice helper listens for a wake word and may save what it hears after that.
  • Students will sort things people say to a voice helper into OK to ask, keep private, and ask a grown-up instead, and give a reason for one choice.
  • Students will catch a wrong or silly answer from a pretend voice helper and name a way to check it.
  • Students will role-play saying "Pip, stop" and turning to a trusted grown-up when a question is about safety, feelings, or buying something.

Học liệu

Trên giấy

  • Handout A: Pip's Script Cards (1 teacher set, cut apart; backs are your key)
  • Handout B: Talk-to-Pip Sort (1 sorting mat and set of strips per group of 3)
  • Handout C: My Smart Speaker Promise (1 per student)
  • A "Pip" prop: a paper cup, tissue box, or cardboard tube with a paper light strip; a green and a red paper circle for Pip's "light"
  • Crayons or pencils

Trên màn hình

  • Optional: a voice assistant on the teacher's device only (a classroom smart speaker, or a phone or tablet assistant with the microphone setting you control)
  • Optional: a document camera or projector to show the sorting mat

Trước khi bắt đầu

  1. Make the Pip prop. Tape a green circle on one side ("I'm listening closely") and a red circle on the other ("I'm waiting for my wake word").
  2. Print Handout A and cut apart the 10 script cards. Put them in order; Cards 1–4 are the teacher demo, Cards 5–10 are for student Pips.
  3. Print and cut Handout B strips for each group of 3. Younger groups can sort with you as a whole class instead.
  4. If you plan the optional live demo, check the device's history or privacy settings first so you can show children where saved recordings appear and how a grown-up can delete them. Do not use a child's name or voice on your own account.

Từng bước

  1. 10–4 phút

    Khởi động

    Who is listening?

    Hold up the Pip prop with the red side showing. Say: "This is Pip. Pip is a pretend talking helper, like a smart speaker or a talking toy. Some of you might have a helper at home that plays songs or tells the weather." Ask for thumbs up if anyone has talked to a helper like this. Then ask: "Is Pip listening right now?" Take guesses. Explain: "Pip's light is red. Pip is waiting for its special word, its wake word. When Pip hears its wake word, the light turns green and Pip listens closely."

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiStay accurate and calm. Helpers wait for a wake word, then listen closely to what comes next; many can save that part. Avoid telling children devices are spying on them all the time.

  2. 24–11 phút

    Làm mẫu

    Meet Pip: three true things

    Use Script Cards 1–4. You are Pip. A volunteer says the wake word, "Hey Pip," and you flip to green. Read Pip's answers from the cards. After each card, name one true thing on the board with a picture: 1. Pip wakes up to a special word. 2. Pip may SAVE what it hears on a computer far away, and grown-ups can look at it or delete it. 3. Pip answers by GUESSING from lots of examples, so it can be wrong. On Card 4, Pip gives a wrong answer with a confident voice ("Spiders have six legs!"). Ask: "Was Pip right? How could we check?"

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiFor Grades 3–5, add: "Pip doesn't understand like a person does. It matches your words to patterns and picks an answer that sounds likely." That is the AI1.1 idea at their level.

  3. 311–19 phút

    Khám phá

    Talk-to-Pip Sort

    Groups of 3 get Handout B. Read each strip aloud (or have readers read). Groups decide where it goes: OK to ask Pip, Keep private, or Ask a grown-up instead. Circulate and ask: "Why did you put it there?" Bring the class back and go over the tricky ones: telling Pip your address, asking Pip to buy something, and telling Pip you feel sad.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiKindergarten: do this as a whole class with three corners of the rug instead of mats. Children walk to the corner that fits.

  4. 419–27 phút

    Luyện tập

    Student Pips

    Volunteers take turns holding Pip. Hand each new Pip one of Script Cards 5–10 to read as Pip's answer. A classmate asks the question on the card. The class decides together: Was that a good thing to ask? Was Pip's answer right? Do we need a grown-up? On the cards where Pip asks a child for private information or wants to buy something, the class practices the power words together: "Pip, stop." Then the child turns to you and says, "Can you help me?"

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiLook for children who notice that Pip sounds sure even when it is wrong. Praise that catch: "You checked instead of just believing!"

  5. 527–31 phút

    Tổng kết

    What did Pip teach us?

    Point to the three true things on the board and read them together. Ask: "When is Pip a good helper?" (songs, timers, simple facts we can check). "When do we go to a person?" (private information, feelings, safety, buying things, when Pip's answer seems wrong). Add a fourth line with the class: 4. I can say "stop" and ask a grown-up.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiIf a child shares that a device at home heard something private, respond warmly and without alarm: "Thanks for telling me. A grown-up at home can check the settings."

  6. 631–35 phút

    Suy ngẫm

    My Smart Speaker Promise

    Children complete Handout C: draw something OK to ask a helper, draw a grown-up they can go to, and finish the sentences. Grades 3–5 also write one question they would ask a grown-up about the helper at home. Ask two or three children to share: "What will you do if a helper asks for your address?"

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiEvidence to collect: children who can say "it might save what I say" and "it can be wrong" in their own words.

Giấy hay màn hình

Không dùng màn hình

The full lesson runs with the Pip prop, printed script cards, and the sorting mat. For a movement version, label three corners of the room OK TO ASK, KEEP PRIVATE, and ASK A GROWN-UP; read each sort strip aloud and children walk to the corner that fits. A child with a green paper circle can stand in for Pip if you have no prop.

Kỹ thuật số

After the Debrief, you may demonstrate a real voice assistant from your own device, in front of the class. Ask it a simple question children can check ("How many legs does a spider have?") and compare its answer to a class book. Then, with the class watching, open the assistant's history or privacy page and show that a recording or text of what you said may be saved, and how a grown-up can delete it. Children do not use their own voices or accounts, and you do not ask it anything personal. Close by asking: "What did the helper keep? Who can delete it?"

Có cần màn hình không? The core ideas land best unplugged: a pretend Pip lets children practice saying "stop" and choosing a grown-up without any real data being saved. A short teacher-driven demo adds evidence paper cannot, because children see with their own eyes that a real helper may keep a record of what it heard.

Bằng chứng học tập

Những gì bạn có thể quan sát hoặc thu thập được nếu hoạt động thành công.

  • Children explain that a voice helper wakes up to a special word and may save what it hears, in their own words.
  • During the sort, children place address, full name, and passwords in Keep private and give a reason ("strangers could know where I live").
  • Children catch Pip's confident wrong answer and name a way to check it (a book, a grown-up, another source).
  • In the role-play, children say "Pip, stop" and turn to a grown-up when Pip asks for private information or tries to buy something.

Điều chỉnh

Kindergarten
Use Script Cards 1–4 and three of the student cards (5, 7, and 9). Sort as a whole class using the three corners. Replace the writing on Handout C with drawing and dictation.
Grades 3–5
Add a "Pip Settings" talk: which settings a family could check (saved recordings, buying by voice, microphone off button). Students write one question to ask a grown-up at home about their device.
Emergent bilinguals
Teach gestures for the three categories (thumbs up for OK, finger to lips for private, pointing to a grown-up for ask a grown-up). Accept answers in any language and pair children with a language buddy for the sort.
Students who use speech devices or AAC
Program or print the phrases "Pip, stop" and "Can you help me?" so every child can take a speaking turn in the role-play.

Suy ngẫm

  • Do I stay safe and make good choices when I talk to a device? What will I keep private? (Student ELE 2.0)
  • How does a voice helper come up with an answer? Why might it be wrong?
  • Who is a grown-up I can go to when a helper's answer seems wrong or a question is about feelings or safety?

Bước tiếp theo

Children take home Handout C and share their promise with a family member. With a grown-up, they can look for the helper's wake word, its microphone off button, and whether it saves what it hears.
