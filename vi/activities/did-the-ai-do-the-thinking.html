Did the AI Do the Thinking?

Students do one thinking task by hand and a matching one with AI, then take a no-notes challenge that reveals what they actually learned, surfacing cognitive offloading, when a tool helps you think and when it thinks instead of you.

In phiếu

Tổng quan

AI can hand you an answer in seconds, which feels like progress but can quietly do the thinking you were supposed to do yourself. This lesson makes that trade visible. Pairs complete two short, similar thinking tasks: one by hand and one where an approved AI does most of the work. Right after, each student faces a short no-notes, no-AI challenge on both topics, and they discover they can usually explain and extend the one they struggled through, but go blank on the one the AI handled. The class names this pattern as cognitive offloading and sorts when offloading is smart (a calculator for arithmetic you already understand) from when it robs you of learning (letting AI reason through something you were trying to learn). The stance is balanced: AI is not banned and not magic. The question students leave with is the one the ELEs ask, what evidence shows the tool improved my learning, not just my output?

Mục tiêu

  • Students will compare a task done by hand with one done mainly by AI and describe the difference in what they can do afterward.
  • Students will define cognitive offloading and give an example of helpful and harmful offloading.
  • Students will judge, for a given task, whether using AI would build or bypass the thinking they need.
  • Students will set one personal rule for when they will do the thinking themselves.

Học liệu

Trên giấy

  • Handout A: Two Tasks (1 per pair; Task 1 done by hand, Task 2 with AI)
  • Handout B: The No-Notes Challenge + My Thinking Rule (1 per student, given out only after both tasks)
  • Handout C: Smart or Sorry Offload? sort (1 per pair, cut apart)

Trên màn hình

  • A district-approved generative AI chatbot for Task 2 only (teacher-driven on a projector for students under 13, or one shared device per pair where permitted)
  • Optional: a document camera to compare pairs' work

Trước khi bắt đầu

  1. Print Handouts A and C per pair/student; keep Handout B hidden until after both tasks are done.
  2. Decide how AI appears for Task 2: teacher-driven on a projector, or one approved shared device per pair. No student accounts.
  3. Do both tasks yourself first so you can predict where the AI will produce a slick but shallow answer.
  4. Set the norm: Task 2 is about letting the AI do the work on purpose, so we can study what that costs.

Từng bước

  1. 10–4 phút

    Khởi động

    The two-second answer

    Ask: "If a tool gives you the right answer in two seconds, did you learn anything?" Take a few votes and reasons. Tell students today they will run an experiment on their own brains: do one task the hard way and one the easy way, then see which one actually stuck. Promise no grade on the tasks themselves, only on what they notice.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiStudents expect to be told AI is bad. Subvert that: frame it as an honest experiment where they are the researchers studying their own learning.

  2. 24–14 phút

    Khám phá

    Task 1, by hand

    Pairs complete Task 1 on Handout A using only their heads, notes, and each other, for example reasoning through why a short argument is flawed, or working out a multi-step word problem and explaining each step. Circulate and listen for the struggle, the false starts, the "wait, no" moments. Tell them that struggle is the brain doing the work.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiChoose a Task 1 that requires reasoning students can do but not instantly. The productive struggle is the point; rescue only if a pair is truly stuck.

  3. 314–22 phút

    Khám phá

    Task 2, let the AI do it

    Now pairs do Task 2, a similar-level task, but this time they ask the approved AI to solve and explain it, and simply copy the result onto Handout A. Tell them explicitly: do not fight the AI or improve it, just let it do the thinking. They will finish fast and feel efficient. Note that feeling out loud: "That was quick. Hold on to how little effort that took."

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiResist the urge to let them critique the AI here; that would re-engage their thinking. The contrast depends on them genuinely offloading this one.

  4. 422–30 phút

    Luyện tập

    The no-notes challenge

    Collect Handout A. Hand out Handout B: a short individual challenge, no notes, no AI, that asks each student to explain the reasoning behind both tasks and solve one small new variation of each. Watch what happens: most can reconstruct Task 1 (the hand one) and stall on Task 2 (the AI one). Let the silence land before the debrief.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiThis is the evidence moment. Keep it low-stakes and private; the goal is self-insight, not embarrassment. Some students will surprise themselves.

  5. 530–39 phút

    Tổng kết

    Name it: cognitive offloading

    Debrief: "Which task could you explain and extend? Which went blank? Why?" Introduce the term cognitive offloading, handing a mental task to a tool. Then pairs run Handout C, sorting examples into smart offload (a map app once you can already read a map, a calculator for arithmetic you understand) and sorry offload (letting AI reason through a problem you were trying to learn to solve). The rule they build: offload what you have already mastered or never needed to learn; keep the thinking that is the actual point of the lesson.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiHold the nuance. Offloading is not evil, overreliance on it for the exact skill you are trying to build is the trap. Let students articulate that line themselves.

  6. 639–45 phút

    Suy ngẫm

    My thinking rule

    On the lower section of Handout B, each student writes one personal rule for AI, for example: "When I'm learning something new, I try it myself first and use AI to check, not to start," or "I never let AI explain something I'll be tested on until I've explained it myself." A few share. Close on the ELE question: "The real test isn't whether the answer is right, it's whether I can now do the thinking myself."

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiPush rules toward when and how, not whether. A rule like "never use AI" is unrealistic; "do the thinking first, then use AI to check" is a habit they can keep.

Giấy hay màn hình

Không dùng màn hình

Run it fully unplugged by replacing the AI in Task 2 with a prepared answer key: pairs copy a complete, correct worked solution from a card instead of generating one. The effect is the same, they offload the thinking to a ready-made answer, then discover on the no-notes challenge that copying did not teach them. The sort and the thinking rule need no devices.

Kỹ thuật số

With devices, the approved AI makes Task 2 feel authentic and fast, which strengthens the contrast with the no-notes challenge. For students under 13, run the AI on a projector; for older students where allowed, one approved shared device per pair, no personal accounts. The point is to let the AI genuinely do the work, then study the cost.

Có cần màn hình không? The digital path earns its place by producing the real, seductive experience of a fast AI answer, so the no-notes challenge can expose what it cost. But because the learning is the contrast, the prepared-answer-key version teaches the same lesson, which is why an unplugged path works just as well.

Bằng chứng học tập

Những gì bạn có thể quan sát hoặc thu thập được nếu hoạt động thành công.

  • On the no-notes challenge, students can explain and extend the hand task better than the AI task, and they notice it.
  • Students correctly define cognitive offloading and place examples into smart versus sorry offload with reasons.
  • Students identify the line: offload mastered or irrelevant steps, keep the thinking a lesson is trying to build.
  • Personal rules specify when and how to use AI, not a blanket yes or no.

Điều chỉnh

Grade 6
Use two short, concrete tasks (a logic puzzle and a similar one) and shorten the no-notes challenge to one explain-it question per task.
9–12
Use discipline-specific tasks (analyzing a primary source, deriving a formula) and add a column on Handout D for which classes this rule matters most in.
Emergent bilinguals
Allow the explanation in the home language and provide the term cognitive offloading with a short gloss and an icon; pair talk can be bilingual.
Students who finish early
Challenge them to redo the AI task the hard way and compare how much they understand afterward, testing their own hypothesis about offloading.

Xem cách tất cả hoạt động được đối chiếu với các chuẩn

Suy ngẫm

  • Which task can you still do on your own, and why do you think that is? (Student ELE 4.3: I am aware of my strengths and areas to improve.)
  • When does a tool help you think, and when does it think instead of you? (AI ELE 4.1: critical thinking about AI-generated information.)
  • What is one kind of thinking you never want to offload, and why? (Student ELE 4.1: I take responsibility for my own learning.)

Bước tiếp theo

Students post their thinking rule in their notebook and apply it on the next assignment that tempts them to let AI start the work. In a follow-up, the class revisits the rules: which held, which were too strict or too loose, and what evidence showed the AI helped learning rather than replacing it.
