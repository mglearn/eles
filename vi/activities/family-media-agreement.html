The Family Media Agreement

A student leads their whole household in building a family media plan the way doctors recommend, covering sleep, meals, content, and screen-free time, so the rules are made with everyone instead of handed to the kids.

Phiếu

  • A What a Good Plan Covers (danh sách kiểm tra)
  • B Run the Family Meeting (phiếu bài tập)
  • C Our Family Media Agreement (phiếu bài tập)

In phiếu

Tổng quan

Most household screen rules are handed down and resented. Pediatric guidance suggests the opposite works better: a family media plan the whole household builds together, one that covers more than minutes, when devices sleep, whether screens come to meals, what content fits which ages, and protected screen-free time, and that holds adults to the same standards as kids. In this activity the student becomes the facilitator. They learn what a good media plan actually covers, practice running a fair family meeting in class, and then lead that meeting at home: gathering everyone's needs, proposing agreements, handling pushback, and writing a plan each person signs. The stance is balanced and realistic: the plan is not a ban list and not a promise that screens are harmless; it is a living agreement a family revisits, built on the idea that people follow rules they helped write. Students practice leadership, negotiation, and the self-advocacy of asking adults to model what they ask of kids.

Mục tiêu

  • Students will identify the parts of a strong family media plan beyond time limits (sleep, meals, content, screen-free time, modeling).
  • Students will facilitate a fair family meeting where every member's needs are heard.
  • Students will negotiate and record agreements the whole household will sign, including ones that apply to adults.
  • Students will build in a review date and explain why a living agreement beats a fixed rule.

Học liệu

Trên giấy

  • Handout A: What a Good Plan Covers, a reference (1 per student)
  • Handout B: Run the Family Meeting, facilitator script and needs-gathering sheet (1 per student, taken home)
  • Handout C: Our Family Media Agreement (1 per student, completed and signed at home)
  • Pens

Trên màn hình

  • Optional: the AAP online Family Media Plan tool, used by a family that prefers a digital version
  • Optional: a shared calendar to set the review-date reminder

Trước khi bắt đầu

  1. Print Handouts A, B, and C for every student.
  2. Model the facilitator stance briefly: a good facilitator asks, listens, and summarizes rather than dictates.
  3. Set the window for the home meeting and the class review day.
  4. Add your name and the return date to the family note on Handout C.

Từng bước

  1. 10–5 phút

    Khởi động

    Rules you didn't help write

    Ask: "Think of a screen rule you've been given that you ignore the second no one's watching. Why?" Collect honest answers (didn't make sense, applied only to me, no one explained it). Then flip it: "What if you ran the meeting that made the rules? Today you learn to lead your family in building a media plan together, one that even applies to the grown-ups."

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiStudents light up at the idea of leading. Channel it toward fair facilitation, not toward writing rules that only benefit themselves.

  2. 25–13 phút

    Khám phá

    What a good plan covers

    Students read Handout A, which lays out what pediatric guidance says a family media plan should cover: device bedtimes (screens out of bedrooms overnight so sleep is protected), mealtimes, content and age-fit, protected screen-free activities, how everyone treats each other online, and, crucially, that adults follow the plan too. In pairs, students rank which parts their own family most needs. Debrief that a plan is much more than a number of minutes.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiThe sleep piece is where evidence is strongest, so highlight device bedtimes. But keep the plan broad: minutes alone is the weakest, most-fought-over lever.

  3. 313–23 phút

    Luyện tập

    Run a practice meeting

    In groups of four, students role-play a family meeting using Handout B's script. One facilitates; others play a tired parent, a younger sibling, and a teen. The facilitator practices the moves: open with purpose, gather each person's needs before proposing anything, propose an agreement, invite pushback, and find a version everyone can accept. Groups swap the facilitator role once so everyone practices leading and being heard.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiCoach the hardest move: gathering needs before proposing solutions. Students, like adults, want to jump to rules; the fair meeting starts with listening.

  4. 423–38 phút

    Vận dụng

    Lead the real meeting at home

    At home, the student facilitates the actual family meeting with Handout B, then records agreements on Handout C across the plan's areas: device bedtimes and where phones charge, mealtime and screen-free times, content and age-fit for different family members, online kindness, and at least one agreement that applies to the adults. Everyone who agrees signs, and the family sets a review date. The student notes one place they had to compromise.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiPrepare students for real pushback and for households that differ (shift work, shared devices, multigenerational homes). A plan that fits the real family beats a textbook-perfect one.

  5. 538–45 phút

    Suy ngẫm

    What held, what you'd change

    Back in class, students debrief in small groups: "What was the hardest need to balance? What agreement surprised you? How did leading the meeting change how your family reacted compared with rules being imposed?" Capture why shared, revisable agreements tend to stick. Close on the balanced idea: a media plan is a living agreement, not a verdict on whether screens are good or bad.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiListen for students realizing that holding adults to the plan was both the hardest and the most powerful part. That mutual accountability is the heart of it.

Giấy hay màn hình

Không dùng màn hình

The whole activity runs on paper: the reference sheet, the role-play script, the needs sheet, and the signed agreement. The family meeting is a face-to-face conversation; no device is required to build or keep the plan.

Kỹ thuật số

A family that prefers it can build the same plan with the AAP online Family Media Plan tool and keep a digital copy, and set the review date as a shared calendar reminder. The in-class role-play and reflection stay unplugged.

Có cần màn hình không? This is paper-first because the learning is facilitation and negotiation among real people. The optional digital tool only stores the plan a family already built together; it adds convenience, not learning.

Bằng chứng học tập

Những gì bạn có thể quan sát hoặc thu thập được nếu hoạt động thành công.

  • Students name plan areas beyond minutes, especially device bedtimes for sleep, and can say why each matters.
  • In the role-play, the facilitator gathers everyone's needs before proposing agreements.
  • The signed agreement covers multiple areas and includes at least one agreement that applies to the adults.
  • The plan has a review date, and the student can explain why a revisable agreement outlasts an imposed rule.

Điều chỉnh

Grade 6
Provide a shorter plan with three areas (device bedtimes, mealtimes, screen-free time) and a simplified meeting script.
9–12
Students facilitate a plan that includes their own greater autonomy and responsibilities, and negotiate a transition toward self-managed limits rather than parent-set ones.
Emergent bilinguals
Offer the family note, script, and agreement in the home language and let the meeting happen in whatever language the family uses.
Nontraditional households
Adapt roles for the real household (one parent, grandparents, siblings, shared custody); the plan can even span two homes with slightly different agreements.

Liên kết với các chuẩn

Students facilitate a family meeting to build a whole-household media plan that applies to adults too, practicing leadership, negotiation, and shared goal-setting.

Suy ngẫm

  • Why do people follow rules they helped write more than rules handed to them? (Student ELE 4.1: I take responsibility for my own learning.)
  • What was it like to lead your family and still make sure everyone was heard? (Student ELE 3.1 and 3.2: teamwork and clear communication.)
  • Why does holding adults to the same plan matter, and how did your family respond to it?

Bước tiếp theo

Families keep the media agreement visible and revisit it on the review date. In class, the facilitation skills carry into student-led conferences or group work, where the same meeting structure, gather needs, propose, invite pushback, agree, helps teams make decisions together.
