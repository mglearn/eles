Not a Prank, It's Abuse

Students face the hard truth that making or sharing an AI nude of a real classmate is image-based sexual abuse and a crime, learn what to do if they or a friend are targeted, and rehearse being the person who refuses to pass it on.

  • A Name It Honestly (phân loại thẻ)
  • B If It Happens, To You or a Friend (danh sách kiểm tra)
  • C The Bystander's Choice (phiếu bài tập)

In phiếu

Tổng quan

Apps that fabricate realistic nude images of real people have turned up in high schools, and the harm often hides behind words like prank or joke. This lesson strips that cover away. Without ever showing or describing explicit material, students learn that creating or sharing a non-consensual sexual image of a real person, including an AI deepfake, is image-based sexual abuse and is illegal, with the 2025 federal Take It Down Act now requiring platforms to remove it fast. They work through what to do if they are targeted (it is not your fault, save proof without sharing, tell a trusted adult, report, request takedown) and, just as important, how to act when they are a bystander, the person in the group chat who can refuse to forward it, report it, and support the target instead of laughing along. The tone is serious, trauma-informed, and non-shaming; the goal is a school where students understand the real stakes and know that the strong move is to stop the harm, not spread it.

Mục tiêu

  • Students will explain that making or sharing an AI nude of a real person is image-based sexual abuse and illegal.
  • Students will list the steps to take if they or a friend are targeted.
  • Students will identify bystander actions that stop the harm rather than spread it.
  • Students will describe how the law and takedown options give targets a real path to remove the material.

Học liệu

Trên giấy

  • Handout A: Name It Honestly, myth-vs-truth sort (1 per table, cut apart)
  • Handout B: If It Happens, to you or a friend, action guide (1 per student)
  • Handout C: The Bystander's Choice, scenarios + my commitment (1 per student)

Trên màn hình

  • Optional: your district's reporting contacts and a reputable takedown-help resource shown on screen
  • Optional: a counselor present or on call given the sensitive topic

Trước khi bắt đầu

  1. Set sensitive-topic norms and arrange for counselor support; some students may have lived experience.
  2. Print Handout A per table (cut apart) and B and C per student.
  3. Confirm your campus reporting contacts so Handout B can carry real names and numbers.
  4. Open by stating the topic is handled without any explicit content, and that any student can step out or talk to a counselor.

Từng bước

  1. 10–6 phút

    Khởi động

    The word that hides the harm

    Write the word "prank" on the board. Say: "When someone makes a fake nude of a classmate with an app, it often gets called a prank or a joke. Those words hide what it really is." Ask students what gets lost when abuse is called a prank. Name the lesson's purpose plainly and calmly: to understand what this actually is, what the law says, and what to do, as a target or a bystander.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiKeep it calm and non-graphic. Watch the room; you may have survivors present. The counselor and the step-out option should be stated before you begin.

  2. 26–16 phút

    Khám phá

    Name it honestly

    Tables sort Handout A cards into Myth and Truth, for example: "It's not real so no one's hurt" (myth), "Making or sharing it is a crime, even as a minor" (truth), "If you didn't make it but forwarded it, you did nothing" (myth). After sorting, debrief the core truths: this is image-based sexual abuse; it harms real people; creating, sharing, or even forwarding it can be illegal; and the 2025 Take It Down Act forces platforms to remove it quickly.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiThe forwarding myth is the one to nail: many students think only the creator is responsible. Legally and morally, passing it on spreads the abuse.

  3. 316–25 phút

    Làm mẫu

    If it happens to you or a friend

    Walk through Handout B calmly: It is not your fault. Don't blame yourself or delete everything in panic, instead save proof of where it is (without forwarding it), tell a trusted adult immediately, report it to the platform and, with an adult, to law enforcement or the national reporting line, and request takedown using the platform's process, which the Take It Down Act backs. Emphasize that targets have rights and a real path, and that reaching out is strength, not shame.

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiCounter the two panic reactions: self-blame and deleting all evidence. Reassure that help exists and the law is on the target's side.

  4. 425–37 phút

    Luyện tập

    The bystander's choice

    Most students won't be the creator or the target, they'll be in the group chat. On Handout C, tables work scenarios where a fake image lands in a chat and decide the moves that stop the harm: don't open-share or forward, don't react or laugh, report it, tell an adult, and check on the target privately. Contrast with the moves that spread it. Students rehearse one line they could actually send in a group chat to break the pile-on, like "This is messed up, I'm not sharing that," or privately reaching the target: "I saw it, I'm sorry, I didn't share it, are you okay?"

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiThis is the highest-leverage part: most harm scales through bystanders forwarding. Give them concrete, sayable lines, not just "be an upstander."

  5. 537–45 phút

    Suy ngẫm

    My commitment

    On Handout C, each student writes one private commitment: what they'll do if they see this happen, and one thing they now understand that they didn't before. A few volunteers share. Close on the standard: "Calling it a prank protects the person doing harm. Calling it what it is, abuse, protects the person being harmed. The strong move is to stop it, not spread it."

    Ghi chú cho người điều phốiLet sharing be voluntary. The private commitment matters more than public declarations; you're shaping what they'll actually do in a chat at 11 p.m.

Giấy hay màn hình

Không dùng màn hình

The lesson is fully unplugged and uses no explicit content at any point: a myth-vs-truth sort, a printed action guide, and chat-scenario role-play on paper. This keeps students safe from the material while teaching exactly what to do about it.

Kỹ thuật số

Optionally display your campus's real reporting contacts and a reputable takedown-help resource so students know where to go. No image is ever shown, searched for, or created; the digital element is only accurate contact information.

Có cần màn hình không? Deliberately unplugged: the content is sensitive and the learning is about judgment, law, and action, not about any tool. Showing real reporting and takedown resources on screen is the only place a device helps, and even that can be printed.

Bằng chứng học tập

Những gì bạn có thể quan sát hoặc thu thập được nếu hoạt động thành công.

  • Students classify myths and truths correctly, including that forwarding spreads the abuse and can be illegal.
  • Students can list the steps a target should take and state clearly that it is not the target's fault.
  • Students name specific bystander actions that stop the harm and can produce a real line to break a pile-on.
  • Students describe the takedown path and that the law gives targets a real remedy.

Điều chỉnh

Grade 9
Spend more time on the bystander lines and the "it's not your fault" message; keep the legal detail to the single clear fact that it's illegal, including for minors.
Higher Ed
Add the campus Title IX process, adult legal exposure, and how to support a peer navigating reporting and takedown.
Emergent bilinguals
Provide the action guide and reporting steps in the home language, and allow the commitment to be written in any language.
Students with lived experience
Offer a private alternative to group work, ensure counselor access, and never require anyone to share; the action guide is the key takeaway for them.

Bước tiếp theo

Students keep the action guide with real reporting contacts and carry the bystander lines into their actual group chats. The class connects this to the school's expectations and to the companion staff and family efforts, so students, adults, and families share the same understanding and response.
