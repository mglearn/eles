The Essential Learning Expectations

TCEA's ELEs give teachers, students, coaches, school leaders, and anyone working with AI a common language for better learning. Each role has five key areas with three indicators each, written as “I know,” “I can,” and “I am aware.” Below each indicator are the activities in this bank that build it.

Teachers & Teacher Librarians

The Essential Learning Expectations for Teachers are designed to guide educators in developing the skills and knowledge necessary for effective, modern teaching. These expectations are grounded in research on best practices in education, including John Hattie's visible learning principles, which emphasize the importance of teacher clarity and feedback. They also incorporate findings from studies on technology integration, culturally responsive teaching, and the development of critical thinking skills. By focusing on areas such as tech integration, digital literacy, inclusive teaching, relationship building, and critical thinking, these ELEs aim to equip teachers with the tools they need to create engaging, effective, and inclusive learning environments that prepare students for their future.

“The most powerful single influence enhancing achievement is feedback.”

John Hattie
Guiding questions
  • What strategies am I using to build positive relationships with my students?
  • How am I fostering critical thinking skills in my lessons?
  • How effectively am I integrating technology into my teaching practices?
  • In what ways am I promoting digital literacy among my students?
  • How am I creating an inclusive learning environment that respects diversity?

Students

The Essential Learning Expectations for Students are designed to prepare learners for success in an increasingly complex and interconnected world. These expectations are based on research in cognitive science, educational psychology, and comprehensive skills frameworks. They draw on studies highlighting the importance of digital literacy, self-regulated learning, and problem-solving skills in academic and future career success. The ELEs for students focus on developing technological competence, online safety awareness, collaborative skills, self-directed learning abilities, and creative problem-solving capabilities. By mastering these competencies, students will be better equipped to navigate the complexities of modern life and become lifelong learners.

“Visible Learning occurs when teachers see learning through the eyes of students and help them become their own teachers.”

John Hattie

  • Do I stay safe and make good choices when I use the internet?

School Leaders & Administrators

The Essential Learning Expectations for School Leaders and Administrators in Technology are designed to guide educational leaders in leveraging technology to create innovative, effective, and future-ready schools. These expectations are grounded in research on technology integration in education, digital leadership, and the impact of educational technology on student achievement. They incorporate findings from studies on data-driven decision making using technology, digital community engagement, and the role of emerging technologies in school improvement. By focusing on visionary technology leadership, technology-enhanced instruction, data-informed decision making with digital tools, fostering a digital learning culture, and technology-enabled community engagement, these ELEs aim to equip school leaders with the knowledge and skills needed to navigate the complex digital landscape of modern education and drive continuous technological improvement in their schools.

“The most important single factor influencing learning is what the learner already knows.”

David Ausubel
Guiding questions
  • What steps am I taking to create an inclusive and positive digital learning culture?
  • How effectively am I leveraging technology to engage with our school community and external stakeholders?
  • How clearly have I articulated and communicated our school's vision for technology integration?
  • In what ways am I supporting and evaluating effective use of educational technology across our school?
  • How am I using digital tools and data analytics to drive school improvement efforts and decision-making?

Instructional Coaches

The Essential Learning Expectations for Instructional Coaches are crafted to enhance the effectiveness of educational support professionals in their critical role of improving teaching and learning. These expectations are rooted in research on adult learning theory, professional development best practices, and the impact of instructional coaching on teacher performance and student outcomes. Drawing from studies on effective coaching models and data-driven decision making in education, these ELEs focus on teacher support, technology integration, data-driven coaching, professional development, and collaborative leadership. By adhering to these expectations, instructional coaches can more effectively guide teachers in improving their practice and ultimately enhance student learning experiences.

“Every student deserves a great teacher, not by chance, but by design.”

John Hattie

  • In what ways am I helping teachers meaningfully integrate technology into their teaching?

AI in Education

The Essential Learning Expectations for AI in Education are designed to guide all stakeholders in the educational system - teachers, students, instructional coaches, and administrators - in the ethical and effective use of AI technologies. These expectations build upon existing educational research and best practices, while addressing the unique challenges and opportunities presented by AI in learning environments.

“The goal of AI is to create machines that can, in turn, create.”

Ray Kurzweil
Guiding questions
  • How am I developing and applying AI literacy in educational contexts?
  • In what ways am I addressing ethical considerations when using AI in learning and teaching?
  • How am I leveraging AI to personalize and enhance learning experiences?
  • What strategies am I employing to promote critical thinking skills when interacting with AI-generated information?
  • How am I fostering collaboration between humans and AI systems in educational projects and research?
